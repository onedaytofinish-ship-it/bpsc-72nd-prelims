Emergency Provisions — Art 352, 356 & 360
📂 Indian Polity ⚖️ Weight: 0.9 🎯 Expected: 2 Qs 📅 Exam: 26 Jul 2026
📚 Core Content & PYQ Alignment
70th BPSC PYQ National Emergency (70th, Q28): BPSC tested Art 352's mechanics — proclamation on the Cabinet's written recommendation; parliamentary approval within 1 month (post-44th Amdt) by special majority; extensions every 6 months indefinitely; grounds: war, external aggression, armed rebellion (44th replaced "internal disturbance"). "Internal disturbance" was the old term — the 1975 Emergency's lesson.
69th BPSC PYQ Financial Emergency (69th, Q118-119): Art 360 tested — financial stability threatened; President's proclamation; parliamentary approval within 2 months by simple majority; no periodic re-approval (continues till revoked); salary reductions possible (including SC/HC judges). Never imposed — India's unused safety valve.

National Emergency — the full mechanics (Art 352)

  • Grounds refined by the 44th (1978): "armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" — the 1975 proclamation's abuse legacy; the President may proclaim even before actual occurrence (imminent danger) — "imminent" language added.
  • Written recommendation: the Cabinet (not merely the PM) must recommend in writing — 1975's oral-advice lesson.
  • Judicial review: 44th made the President's satisfaction reviewable on mala fides; Minerva Mills (1980): the "satisfaction" is not purely political.
  • LS's special power: Art 352(7)-(8): 1/10 of LS members may require a special sitting to consider revocation — the minority-trigger device (44th).
  • Effects: Centre's executive extends to states' subjects; Parliament legislates on State List (Art 250); LS's term extendable 1 year at a time; financial redistribution (Art 354); Art 355's duty basis.
  • Art 358 vs 359: 358 = Art 19 automatically suspended (only war/external aggression); 359 = enforcement of named FRs suspended by presidential order — but never Arts 20-21 (the 44th's codified repudiation of ADM Jabalpur, which Puttaswamy formally overruled in 2017).
Rashtrapati Bhavan — the President proclaims all three emergencies (Arts 352, 356, 360), but always on the Union Cabinet's written advice (Art 352 post-44th) and subject to Parliament's approval.

President's Rule (Art 356) — the most-used weapon

  • Trigger: "government cannot be carried on per the Constitution" — President's satisfaction on the Governor's report or otherwise.
  • S.R. Bommai (1994): judicial review; floor test; no dissolution before parliamentary approval; secularism/basic-structure shield; majority-proclamation abuse curbed.
  • Effects (Arts 356-357): state assembly dissolved/suspended; President assumes state executive; Parliament (or the President when Parliament is not in session, subject to approval) exercises the state's legislative powers; Governor acts on the Union's directions.
  • Max 3 years with the 44th's brakes: beyond 1 year only if (a) National Emergency in force, or (b) EC certifies elections can't be held.
  • Bihar's tryst: imposed 8+ times — 1995 (short-lived), 1999 (Rabri Devi govt), and the 2005 attempt struck down (Rameshwar Prasad — Topic 45).

Emergency timeline — the constitutional flow

Cabinet's written recommendation → President proclaims (Art 352)
Parliament approval within 1 month — special majority (both Houses)
Continues 6 months → extensions every 6 months indefinitely (no max)
Revocation: President anytime; or LS resolution (1/10 members' demand for sitting)

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. [70th BPSC] A proclamation of National Emergency under Article 352 can be made only on the written recommendation of:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — 44th Amdt: the Union Cabinet's written recommendation is mandatory (1975's oral-advice lesson). The PM alone cannot trigger it — the whole Cabinet must be on record.
Q2. The grounds for National Emergency after the 44th Amendment include:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — 44th (1978): "armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" (too vague, abused in 1975). War/external aggression/armed rebellion — and the President may act even on imminent danger.
Q3. A proclamation of National Emergency must be approved by Parliament within:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — 44th: 1 month (was 2 months), special majority, both Houses; otherwise it lapses. Extensions: every 6 months indefinitely — each needs fresh approval.
Q4. National Emergency has been proclaimed in India:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 3 times: 1962 (China war), 1971 (Pak war), 1975 (internal disturbance — the only non-war one; revoked 1977).
Q5. Financial Emergency under Article 360:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 360: never imposed; approval within 2 months by simple majority; no maximum duration — continues till revoked. 69th BPSC (Q118-119) tested exactly this never-used status.
Q6. Under Art 358, Article 19 is automatically suspended:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 358: Art 19 stands suspended only for war/external aggression emergencies — the 44th's refinement (1975-type internal emergencies no longer touch Art 19). Art 359 is the separate order-based route.
Q7. Which Fundamental Rights can NEVER be suspended even during an Emergency?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 44th Amdt: Arts 20-21 non-suspendable under any presidential order (Art 359's bar) — the anti-ADM-Jabalpur codification (that 1976 case was formally overruled in Puttaswamy, 2017).
Q8. President's Rule under Art 356 can last a maximum of:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — 6 months at a time, extendable to 3 years max; beyond 1 year only if a National Emergency is in force or the EC certifies elections can't be held (44th's brakes).
Q9. S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) established:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Bommai (1994): floor test; judicial review; assembly dissolution only after Parliament approves; secularism = basic structure. Bihar's Rameshwar Prasad (2006) applied it to strike the dissolution.
Q10. The 1975 National Emergency was proclaimed on the ground of:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — June 1975: "internal disturbance" (the pre-44th term) — triggered amid JP's Bihar-rooted movement; the 44th later renamed the ground "armed rebellion" precisely to prevent such use.
Q11. The Lok Sabha may require reconsideration of a National Emergency by a special sitting if:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 352(8)-(9) (44th): 1/10 of LS members may demand a special sitting to consider revocation; if passed by simple majority, the Emergency stands revoked — LS's anti-emergency check.
Q12. During a National Emergency, the Lok Sabha's term can be extended:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — LS term: extendable 1 year at a time under a National Emergency (not beyond 6 months after it ends). State assemblies: 6 months at a time (Art 172).
Q13. Under Art 360, during a Financial Emergency, the President may direct:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 360: salary-reduction directions (including judges' pensions/salaries per 360(4)(b)); money-bills reservation for the President; financial propriety observance. The judge-salary clause is the classic MCQ point.
Q14. Bihar's Assembly dissolution (2005) was struck down because:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Rameshwar Prasad (2006): Governor Buta Singh's recommendation was mala fide (no floor test, no chance to form a government) — Bommai principles applied to Bihar's 2005 dissolution.
Q15. Under Art 359, the President may suspend the enforcement of:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 359: presidential order suspends enforcement of specified FRs (not the rights themselves); Arts 20-21 exempt (44th). Art 358 is automatic; 359 needs an order naming rights.
Q16. Art 365 deals with:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 365: a state's failure to comply with the Union's executive directions = deemed "failure of constitutional machinery" → Art 356 available — the backdoor trigger.
Q17. The proclamation of President's Rule must be approved by Parliament within:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 356: approval within 2 months by simple majority (both Houses); continues 6 months per approval, max 3 years. Contrast 352's 1-month/special-majority rule.
Q18. During a National Emergency, the Centre's executive power extends to:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 353: the Union may direct states on any matter; Parliament may legislate on State List subjects (Art 250) — the "unitary conversion" of the federation (Art 353/250/354 combo).
Q19. Which state experienced President's Rule when Jharkhand was carved out of it (2000)?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Bihar was under President's Rule (1999-2000, the Rabri Devi dismissal's aftermath) when the Bihar Reorganisation Act 2000 created Jharkhand (Nov 15, 2000) — a rare reorganisation-during-356 episode.
Q20. The 44th Amendment's emergency-package changes include all EXCEPT:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The 44th ADDED the LS's 1/10-members special-sitting + simple-majority revocation (Art 352(7)-(8)) — it did not remove it. A/B/C are the 44th's actual signature changes.
Q21. 'Samvidhan Hatya Diwas' commemorates:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — June 25 declared Samvidhan Hatya Diwas (2024) marking the 1975 Emergency's proclamation — the 50th anniversary fell in June 2025, keeping the topic exam-fresh.
Q22. During President's Rule in a state, the dissolved/suspended assembly's fate and Parliament's role:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — During 356: Parliament (or the President, when Parliament not in session, subject to approval) exercises the state's legislative powers; Bommai: no dissolution before Parliament approves the proclamation.
Q23. Who decides whether a National Emergency proclamation deserves revocation?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Dual revocation: the President's revocation anytime (no approval needed) + LS's resolution route (1/10 demand → special sitting within 14 days → simple majority = revoked).
Q24. The JP Movement (1974) and the 1975 Emergency are linked because:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — JP's Bihar-born movement (1974 Gujarat + Bihar agitations → Total Revolution) + the June 1975 Allahabad HC verdict = the Emergency's immediate triggers; JP was arrested (Detention era) — the Bihar-history/polity crossover BPSC loves.
Q25. Financial Emergency directions under Art 360(4) include all EXCEPT:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 360(4): salary-reduction directions + money-bill reservation — NOT the FC's dissolution (the FC is a constitutional body under Art 280; no emergency power touches it).
