3️⃣ President's Rule — Bihar's eight-plus episodes
  • 1968-69: after Mahamaya Prasad Sinha's ministry fell — Bihar's early taste of 356.
  • 1977-80 (Janata wave collapse) and 1980: post-Charan Singh-era impositions.
  • 1995: Kedar Pandey ministry's brief fall — quickly reversed.
  • 1999-2000: Rabri Devi's dismissal (Vajpayee-era) — the long spell through the Jharkhand bifurcation (Nov 2000).
  • 2005 (twice): February's unconstitutional dissolution (Rameshwar Prasad) + November's post-election spell.
  • The takeaway for MCQs: Bihar = a 356 "repeat customer" pre-Bommai; post-Bommai (1994) and Rameshwar Prasad (2006), impositions require floor tests — none on Bihar since 2005.
JP & the Emergency — the Bihar thread
  • 1974: JP's movement born of Bihar's student agitation (Gujarat's parallel) — anti-corruption Total Revolution.
  • 1975: JP's "sangharsh sena" call + Allahabad verdict = proclamation's immediate context.
  • Detention: JP arrested at Gandhi Maidan's era of protest — MISA's most famous prisoner.
  • 1977: JP's post-Emergency moral authority installed the Janata government — "the Loknayak effect."
  • Exam link: Topic 157 (Modern Bihar) frames the movement; Topic 46 frames the constitutional damage + repair.
4️⃣ Financial Emergency — the never-used valve
  • Trigger: "financial stability or credit of India or any part threatened" — the President's satisfaction (no written-Cabinet bar unlike 352).
  • Approvals: 2 months, simple majority — no max duration, no re-approvals (the loosest of the three — hence the 2025 debate on "should it be tightened?").
  • Powers: salary directions (Union + state + judges); state money-bill reservation; financial-propriety directions.
  • Near-misses: 1991 Balance-of-Payments crisis — considered, not used; COVID-2020 chatter — again unused. "Never imposed" is the exam-stable fact.
  • Revocation: President anytime (simple instrument); no LS trigger like 352.
