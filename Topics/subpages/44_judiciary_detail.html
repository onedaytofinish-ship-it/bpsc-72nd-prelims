2️⃣ The Five Writs — complete drill
WritLiteralPurposeAgainstKey point
Habeas corpus"You may have the body"Produce a detainee before the courtPublic + private detentionThe only writ against private parties; against unlawful detention
Mandamus"We command"Compels a public dutyPublic officials/bodies (not the President/Governors; not private)Duty must be statutory/public — no discretionary-appeal
Prohibition"To forbid"Stops a lower court/tribunal exceeding jurisdictionSubordinate courts/tribunalsPreventive — before the order completes
Certiorari"To certify"Quashes an order already passedSubordinate courts/tribunals (now also quasi-judicial bodies post-Gopalan)Curative — after the order
Quo warranto"By what authority"Questions a person's claim to a public officePublic office-holdersOffice must be substantive/public; no private offices
BPSC Pattern Prohibition vs Certiorari is BPSC's favourite writ pair — preventive vs curative. Also: mandamus lies against judicial bodies too when refusing jurisdiction; habeas cannot be refused on procedural technicality (epistolary relaxation).
3️⃣ PIL Landmarks & Bihar's Role
  • 1979 — Hussainara Khatoon: Bihar undertrials; speedy trial + free legal aid as rights (Art 21).
  • 1980 — Mumbai Kamgar Sabha: locus standi relaxation articulated (Bhagwati J).
  • 1981 — S.P. Gupta: PIL formally legitimised ("public-spirited person").
  • 1986 — MC Mehta: absolute liability (oleum gas); Ganga pollution PIL series → environmental jurisprudence.
  • 1993 — Vishaka: harassment guidelines till legislation (1997) — law-declaration under Art 141.
  • 1997 — Puttaswamy precursor line → 2017 privacy judgment: 9-judge bench; Art 21 includes privacy.
  • Guidelines era: Court fills legislative vacuum (Vishaka model) — then Parliament's law replaces.
Bihar & PIL — the meta-stories
  • Hussainara (1979): the case that proved PIL's power came from Bihar's jails — 40,000+ undertrials exposed; release on personal bonds ordered.
  • Patna HC's contemporary docket: caste-survey validation (2023), liquor prohibition challenges (2024-25), teacher-recruitment litigation (2024-25) — BPSC can flip these into Art 226 illustrations.
  • BPSC cases in courts: appointment disputes heard at Patna HC — the Commission's own legal environment.
4️⃣ Tribunalisation & L. Chandra Kumar
  • Arts 323A/323B (42nd Amdt): administrative + other tribunals — created to reduce court burden.
  • Sampat Kumar (1987) → L. Chandra Kumar (1997): tribunal decisions subject to HC (Art 226/227) + SC judicial review — high courts cannot be replaced; judicial review = basic structure.
  • Tribunals Reforms Act 2021: abolished some tribunals (film certification, IPAB etc.) — a 2024-25 current-affairs echo.
  • NCLT/NCLAT, CAT, NGT — the surviving architecture; NGT (2010) is BPSC's environment crossover.
