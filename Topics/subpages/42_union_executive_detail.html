2️⃣ Statics — Discretionary Space & Positions

Where the President still has discretion (post-44th)

  • Appointing the PM: when no party has a majority — the President chooses (e.g., 1979 Charan Singh; 1989 V.P. Singh; 1996 Vajpayee's 13-day govt; 1998, 2004, 2009 — incumbent-PM convention evolved later).
  • Dismissing the CoM: if the LS majority is lost and the PM refuses to resign (rare; no Indian instance yet).
  • Dissolving the LS: if the CoM advises dissolution against the House's confidence.
  • Returning advice once (44th Amendment) — the sole formal reconsideration right.
  • Sitting on mercy petitions — courts have pushed for timelines, but no constitutional clock (Epuru Sudhakar permits judicial review on narrow grounds).

3️⃣ Background & Bihar Connection
Bihar's Art 356 & succession stories
  • Rajendra Prasad (Siwan): first & longest-serving President (1950–62); the 1951/1957 elections; his differences with Nehru (Art 74 conventions) are famous constitutional-history notes.
  • President's Rule in Bihar: imposed 8+ times — 1967, 1968, 1969, 1972, 1977, 1980, 1995, 1999, and the 2005 double dissolution — the March 2005 dissolution was declared unconstitutional in Rameshwar Prasad v. UOI (2006), SC's landmark controlling Art 356 abuse — a Bihar case BPSC can frame directly.
  • Bihar's Legislative Council: created 1911-era (bicameral from 1937 elections); Bihar assembly resolved to abolish it (2020) — Art 169's pending parliamentary step.
  • Cabinet Biharis: Jagjivan Ram (Defence during 1971 war), later Railway/Defence ministers from Bihar illustrate the CoM's composition diversity.
