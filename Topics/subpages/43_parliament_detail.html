Parliament — Deep Dive: Sessions, Privileges, 106th Amendment & Predictions
1️⃣ Core Concepts — Sessions & Privileges

Sessions, summons, recesses

  • Six months rule (Art 85): not more than 6 months between two sittings — Parliament must meet at least twice a year.
  • Three usual sessions: Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (Jul–Sep), Winter (Nov–Dec).
  • Summoning: by the President; prorogation ends a session (pending bills don't lapse); dissolution ends the LS's life (bills lapse per rules).
  • Adjournment vs adjournment sine die: presiding officer's call; sine die = indefinite (until reconvened).
  • Joint sitting (Art 108): 3 historical — Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961), Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (1978), POTA Bill (2002).
  • President's Address (Art 87): first session each year + the first after each general election — no debate on the address itself is the trap; motion of thanks follows.
  • Lame-duck session: the LS's last sitting before dissolution — members facing exit.

Privileges & immunities (Arts 105, 194)

BPSC Pattern Privileges are not codified: BPSC tests that parliamentary privileges remain largely uncodified (House = court of its own privileges; Searchlight case, 1959). The 15th LS's committee drafted a draft, never enacted. Judicial review exists for mala fide/expulsion cases (Raja Ram Pal, 2007).

Bihar & Parliament
