Fundamental Rights — Articles 12–35, Writs & Landmark Cases
📂 Indian Polity ⚖️ Weight: 0.9 🎯 Expected: 2–3 Qs 📅 Exam: 26 Jul 2026
📚 Core Content & PYQ Alignment
71st BPSC PYQ Reasonable restrictions (71st, Q65): The 71st asked a 4-statement question on "reasonable restrictions" on Fundamental Rights — testing that reasonableness must consider the interests of the general public, and that restrictions are judicially reviewable. Also repeatedly tested: concurrent-list subjects (Q59) and schedules matching (Q62). Master the FR-vs-restriction balance for multi-statement traps.
69th BPSC PYQ Untouchability & Fifth Schedule (69th, Q105/107): The 69th asked directly: "Untouchability is abolished by which Article?" — Article 17; and "The Fifth Schedule deals with the governance and protection of which group?" — Scheduled Tribes (Scheduled Areas). Single-fact FR questions are BPSC's bread and butter: memorise the Article-to-right table cold.

Six Fundamental Rights — the complete map (Part III, Arts 12–35)

RightArticlesKey features/traps
Equality14, 15, 16, 17, 18Art 14: equality before law + equal protection; Art 17: abolishes untouchability (absolute — no exceptions); Art 18: abolishes titles (awards like Bharat Ratna are NOT titles)
Freedom19, 20, 21, 21A, 22Art 19: six freedoms; Art 21: life & personal liberty (Maneka Gandhi 1978 broadened); Art 21A: education 6–14 yrs (86th Amendment 2002); Art 22: arrest safeguards
Against Exploitation23, 24Art 23: bans human trafficking & forced labour (State may impose compulsory service — e.g., military/social); Art 24: no child below 14 in hazardous employment
Freedom of Religion25, 26, 27, 28Art 25: conscience + profess/practice/propagate; Art 27: no compulsory religious taxes; Art 28: no religious instruction in wholly State-funded institutions
Cultural & Educational29, 30Art 29: any section's language/culture; Art 30: minorities to establish educational institutions (not confined to citizens)
Constitutional Remedies32Dr. Ambedkar: "heart and soul of the Constitution"; 5 writs; SC original but not exclusive jurisdiction; FRs are defended but not defined
Mnemonic — order of FRs: E-F-E-R-C-C ("Every Fellow Enjoys Rights, Clearly & Completely") — Equality (14–18) → Freedom (19–22) → Exploitation (23–24) → Religion (25–28) → Cultural-Educational (29–30) → Constitutional Remedies (32). Note the gaps: Art 31 (property) deleted by 44th Amendment 1978; Art 21A inserted; Art 22A-30 no.

The Supreme Court of India — guardian of Fundamental Rights. Article 32 gives it original (though not exclusive) jurisdiction to issue writs for FR enforcement; "heart and soul" per Dr. Ambedkar.

Landmark cases — the FR evolution ladder

A.K. Gopalan (1950): Art 21 read narrowly — "procedure established by law" means any enacted procedure
Shankari Prasad (1951) → Golak Nath (1967) → Kesavananda (1973): FRs amendable vs basic-structure battle
ADM Jabalpur (1976): FRs suspended during Emergency (widely criticised) → overruled in Puttaswamy (2017)
Maneka Gandhi (1978): procedure must be fair, just, reasonable; golden triangle Arts 14-19-21
Puttaswamy (2017): privacy is intrinsic to Art 21 (9-judge bench, overruling ADM Jabalpur & M.P. Sharma)

The Constitution's illuminated pages — Part III (Arts 12–35) carries the six Fundamental Rights. The original also bore Art 31 (property), deleted by the 44th Amendment in 1978.
Bihar Connection 🔗
  • Art 17 in Bihar's soil: Bihar's Champaran (1917) and later anti-caste movements (Karpoori Thakur's reservations, 1978 — first OBC quota in India) operationalised Art 17's promise. The Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955 (renamed Protection of Civil Rights Act 1976) had early test cases from Bihar.
  • Art 23 & Bihar: historically, migrant-bonded labour flows from Bihar (e.g., brick-kilns of Punjab/Haryana) made Art 23's forced-labour ban a Bihar-relevant enforcement story; SC's Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976 jurisprudence drew on such cases.
  • Art 21A & Bihar: post-2002, Bihar's school-enrollment drives (Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan, bicycle-scheme for girls) are RTE-linked examples BPSC could use as distractors.
  • Patna High Court (est. 1916) is the FR guardian at state level — writs under Art 226 (power exceeds Art 32: covers "any other purpose" too, not just FRs).
  • Art 19 & JP's Bihar: the 1974 Bihar movement (Jayaprakash Narayan) invoked freedoms of expression/assembly — and the subsequent Emergency (1975–77, see Topic 46) suspended them, making the JP story Bihar's own FR parable.

🆕 Current Relevance 2024–26
  • Property not FR but 300A debates (2024–25): SC observations in eviction/property cases reaffirm Art 300A's status as constitutional (not fundamental) right — "right to property" revival demands (some MPs) keep this fresh; exam point: 44th Amendment, 1978.
  • Privacy → new derivative rights (post-Puttaswamy): the SC continues to derive rights from Art 21 — internet access (Anuradha Bhasin), choice of food, shelter; 2024–25 judgments on digital arrests/online fraud reference Art 21 dignity.
  • Article 19(1)(a) & digital speech: SC/HC rulings on social-media takedowns and IT Rules 2021 challenges (ongoing 2024–25) test "procedure, fairness" — Maneka-era doctrine now applied online.
  • EWS & Art 15(6): 103rd Amendment upheld (Janhit Abhiyan, Nov 2022) — 10% EWS quota constitutional; follow-up litigation on implementation (2024–25) keeps Art 15 amendments relevant.
  • Bihar angle: Patna HC's 2024–25 PIL rulings on flood-relief, liquor-policy and teacher-recruitment cases regularly test Art 14 arbitrareness & Art 21 livelihood — a good source of BPSC-style scenario questions.

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. [69th BPSC] Untouchability is abolished by which Article of the Constitution?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Article 17 abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form; it is absolute (no "reasonable restrictions" clause). Enforcing law: Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955; SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989. Art 14 is general equality; Art 15 prohibits specific discriminations; Art 22 deals with arrest.
Q2. Which Article did Dr. B.R. Ambedkar call the "heart and soul of the Constitution"?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) — "the heart and soul" per Ambedkar, because without a remedy the rights are meaningless. It is itself a Fundamental Right; SC's jurisdiction is original but not exclusive (HCs under Art 226).
Q3. The right to property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The 44th Amendment (1978) deleted Art 31; property is now a constitutional/legal right under Art 300A (Part XII). The 42nd had earlier curtailed property rights; 52nd is anti-defection; 86th added Art 21A (education).
Q4. Article 21A, making education a fundamental right for children aged 6–14, was added by:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — 86th Amendment (2002) inserted Art 21A; operationalised by the RTE Act 2009 (in force 1 Apr 2010). 93rd Amendment (2005) amended Art 15(5) for OBC/SC/ST reservation in private unaided institutions.
Q5. Which of the following Fundamental Rights are available ONLY to citizens of India (not foreigners)?
1. Article 15  2. Article 16  3. Article 19  4. Article 21
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Arts 15, 16, 19 (also 29, 30) are citizens-only. Art 21 (life & personal liberty) — like Arts 14, 20, 22–28 — extends to all persons including foreigners. BPSC's favourite availability trap.
Q6. Match the writs with their purposes:

(a) Habeas Corpus (b) Mandamus (c) Certiorari (d) Quo Warranto
1) Command to perform public duty 2) Produce a detained person 3) Test claim to a public office 4) Quash an order without jurisdiction
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Habeas Corpus → produce the detained body; Mandamus → "we command" performance of duty; Certiorari → quash (post-decision); Quo Warranto → "by what authority" do you hold office. Prohibition (not listed) stops before the decision.
Q7. The "golden triangle" of the Constitution refers to Articles:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — The golden triangle: Arts 14 + 19 + 21 — laws must satisfy all three together (Maneka Gandhi, 1978; K.S. Puttaswamy reaffirmed). Options B/C/D are jurisdictional/administrative article sets.
Q8. In Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978), the Supreme Court held that the "procedure" under Article 21 must be:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Maneka Gandhi overruled A.K. Gopalan's narrow reading: procedure under Art 21 must be fair, just and reasonable (due process substance), and Art 21 must be read with Arts 14 & 19. Option A was Gopalan (1950) — the old view.
Q9. In Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017), the Supreme Court:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — A 9-judge bench unanimously held privacy is intrinsic to Art 21 (and Part III generally), overruling M.P. Sharma & Kharak Singh's contrary indications and expressly overruling ADM Jabalpur (option D is the opposite). Aadhaar Act was later (2018) upheld with modifications — not struck down entirely.
Q10. Which of the following is/are grounds NOT available for restricting Article 19(1)(a) (free speech)?
1. Contempt of court  2. Friendly relations with foreign states  3. Inadequate traffic infrastructure  4. Incitement to an offence
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 19(2) lists 8 grounds: sovereignty & integrity, security of State, friendly relations, public order, decency/morality, contempt, defamation, incitement. "Traffic infrastructure" (3) is not a ground — the classic absurd-option trap. 1, 2 and 4 are all valid.
Q11. Article 33 of the Constitution empowers which body to restrict Fundamental Rights of members of the armed forces?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 33: Parliament (by law) may restrict FRs of armed forces, para-military, police etc. to ensure discipline and proper performance of duties. Art 34 (martial law indemnity) is also Parliament's domain. The President acts on advice; courts do not restrict FRs.
Q12. During a National Emergency (Art 352), which Fundamental Rights CANNOT be suspended even by Presidential order (after the 44th Amendment)?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — The 44th Amendment (1978) made Arts 20 & 21 non-suspendable even during Emergency (Art 359 orders cannot touch them). Art 19 is suspendable only in war/external emergency (Art 358, not armed rebellion). ADM Jabalpur's premise died with this amendment.
Q13. Certiorari and Prohibition differ in that:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Prohibition stops a lower court/tribunal while proceedings are pending; certiorari quashes an order already made. Option A reverses them (classic swap trap). Certiorari can also issue against administrative authorities (post-1991 expansion); prohibition is essentially judicial.
Q14. The right to "propagate" religion under Article 25 means:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Propagate = transmit tenets; the SC (Rev. Stainislaus, 1977) held it does NOT include a right to convert another forcibly (option A). Art 25 belongs to "all persons" (option D wrong). Art 28(1) forbids compulsory religious instruction in wholly State-funded institutions (option C wrong).
Q15. Under Article 15, the State CANNOT make special provision for:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 15(1) prohibits discrimination on grounds ONLY of religion (etc.) — so "special provisions for a religious community as such" is impermissible. 15(3) women/children, 15(4) SEBC/SC/ST, 15(5) OBC in institutions, 15(6) EWS (103rd Amendment, upheld in Janhit Abhiyan 2022) are all valid exceptions.
Q16. Article 18 (abolition of titles) does NOT prohibit:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 18(4) bars acceptance of foreign titles by citizens without President's consent; Art 18(1)-(2) bar State titles (Bharat Ratna etc. were held to be awards, not "titles" — Balaji Raghavan, 1996). Military/academic distinctions are expressly exempt.
Q17. Consider the following statements about Article 22:
1. It protects against arrest & detention in criminal cases.
2. Detention beyond 24 hours requires a Magistrate's approval.
3. Preventive detention safeguards are in Art 22(3)–(7).
4. Detention under preventive-detention law can be advisory-board-reviewed beyond 3 months.
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — All four correct: Art 22(1)-(2) arrest safeguards (ground communication, counsel, 24-hour production); 22(3)-(7) preventive detention (advisory board report beyond 3 months — Art 22(4)). Note: Parliament's preventive-detention law supremacy (Art 22(7)) — a BPSC favourite.
Q18. The writ jurisdiction of High Courts under Article 226 differs from the Supreme Court's under Article 32 in that:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 226 extends to FRs and other legal rights ("any other purpose") — wider in scope; but Art 32 is itself an FR and SC's remedy can't be refused on discretion the way HC writs can. Option D reverses: Art 32 is the FR, not 226.
Q19. In Kesavananda Bharati (1973), the Supreme Court held that Fundamental Rights:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Kesavananda overruled Golak Nath (1967, which had made FRs unamendable — option A): Parliament can amend FRs (including by Art 31 changes) but cannot destroy the Constitution's basic structure. Option C: post-Minerva Mills (1980), FRs & DPSPs are complementary, not hierarchical. Option D is a special-procedure red herring.
Q20. Which Article deals with the abolition of forced/bonded labour?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 23 prohibits human trafficking and begar/forced labour (Bonded Labour System Abolition Act 1976 implements it). Art 24 prohibits child labour below 14 in hazardous jobs. The State MAY impose compulsory service for public purposes (military/social service) — an express exception.
Q21. "Equality before the law" in Article 14 is borrowed from ____ while "equal protection of the laws" is from ____.
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — "Equality before law" (a negative concept — no special privileges; Dicey's Rule of Law) ← UK; "equal protection of laws" (a positive concept — like treated alike) ← USA (14th Amendment). Together they make Art 14.
Q22. Which of the following is a correct pair (case — principle)?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Maneka Gandhi (1978) = fair-just-reasonable procedure. A: privacy is Puttaswamy (2017); C: ADM Jabalpur held the opposite (suspended — later overruled); D: Minerva Mills (1980) struck the 42nd's attempt to make FRs amendable at will, restoring basic-structure review.
Q23. Article 30 (right of minorities to establish educational institutions) is available to:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 30 covers minorities based on religion or language (not race) — any section of citizens. "Minority" is state-level determined (e.g., Sikhs in Bihar are a minority; Hindus in Punjab). Majority-rights claims fail under Art 30 (option D).
Q24. Consider the statements regarding Art 19(1)(g):
1. It guarantees the right to practise any profession or carry on any occupation, trade or business.
2. The State can impose reasonable restrictions in the interest of the general public.
3. The State may create monopolies in its favour (complete exclusion).
4. Professional/technical qualifications may be prescribed.
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — All four correct: 19(1)(g) guarantee; 19(6) allows (i) general-public interest restrictions, (ii) professional/technical qualifications, and (iii) State monopoly/exclusion from any business — the express "complete exclusion" clause. Liquor-ban cases (Bihar prohibition) are tested here.
Q25. Which statement about the 103rd Amendment (2019) is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — The 103rd Amendment (Jan 2019) inserted Arts 15(6) & 16(6) for 10% EWS quota — upheld in Janhit Abhiyan (Nov 2022). Option C is the 102nd Amendment (2018, NCBC); B & D are unrelated. EWS excludes OBC/SC/ST/SEBC beneficiaries.
