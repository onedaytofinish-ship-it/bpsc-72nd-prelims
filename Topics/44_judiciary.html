📚 Core Content & PYQ Alignment
69th BPSC PYQ Collegium composition (69th, Q1-3 band): BPSC tested the judges-appointment mechanism: the Collegium for SC judges = CJI + 4 senior-most SC judges (for HC judges: CJI + 2 senior-most + the HC's Chief Justice). The 99th Amendment/NJAC (2014) sought to replace it but was struck down (4:1, 2015) — collegium survives. Supreme Court — architecture (Arts 124–147)

  • Art 124: SC = CJI + 33 other judges (Parliament-fixed; original 7, raised over time — 34 total now).
  • Appointment: Collegium — CJI + 4 senior-most puisne judges for SC; no veto for the executive (only a one-time return; must re-accept if resent).
  • Qualification: citizen + HC judge 5 years / HC advocate 10 years / distinguished jurist (Art 124(3)); no minimum age.
  • Tenure: till 65 (SC) / 62 (HC); removal only by the President on an address by both Houses (special majority) for "proved misbehaviour or incapacity" (Art 124(4)) — Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968: motion → Speaker/Chairman admits (motion of 100 MPs) → 3-member committee (SC judge + HC CJ + distinguished jurist).
  • Art 129: SC = court of record (contempt powers).
  • Art 131: original jurisdiction — Centre-state, state-state disputes (exclusive, but excludes matters with interim remedies).
  • Art 132-136: appellate jurisdiction — constitutional (cert from HC), civil (Art 133 — substantial question of law), criminal (Art 134 — death/LC cases), special leave (Art 136 — discretionary, any court/tribunal).
  • Art 141: SC's law binding on all Indian courts.
  • Art 143: advisory jurisdiction — President may refer; SC may report (duty if pre-constitutional treaties etc.); not binding on the President.
Supreme Court of India — Art 124: CJI + 33 judges; the final interpreter and guardian of the Constitution (basic structure since 1973).

High Courts & subordinate judiciary (Arts 214–237)

  • Art 214: HC for each state; common HCs for states (Art 231) — e.g., Bombay, Guwahati.
  • HC judges: appointment by President after collegium consultation; removal same SC-grade process (Art 218 mirrors 124(4)); transfer by President (collegium advice, not "consultation" with the CJ — SC clarified in 2nd/Nevsun).
  • Art 225: jurisdiction & powers as before the Constitution (predecessor-court continuity).
  • Art 226: writs for FRs and any other legal right — "wider" than Art 32; discretionary; 227 superintendence over subordinate courts/tribunals (not military).
  • Art 233-237: district judges — appointment by the Governor in consultation with the HC (Art 233) — the Shetty (1992) rule: no executive discretion.

PIL & epistolary jurisdiction

  • Origin: US social-action litigation adapted — S.P. Gupta v Union of India (1981) legitimised locus standi relaxation; Justice P.N. Bhagwati & V.R. Krishna Iyer as architects.
  • Relaxations: any public-spirited person/petition (even a letter — epistolary); no strict locus standi; court's duty to protect the voiceless.
  • Landmark PILs: Hussainara Khatoon (speedy trial, Bihar — prisoners!), Sunil Batra (solitary confinement), Olga Tellis (pavement dwellers), MC Mehta (environment — Ganga), Vishaka (harassment guidelines), Puttaswamy (privacy).
  • Criticism: judicial overreach/activism debates — the balance: PIL is a jurisdictional innovation, not a substitute for governance.

Bihar Connection 🔗
  • Patna High Court (1916): one of India's oldest HCs; the 1912 separation-era demand crystallised with the 1916 charter — BPSC's HC-anniversary staple (2026 = 110th year). Its writ jurisdiction (Art 226) serves Bihar's PIL tradition.
  • Hussainara Khatoon (1979): the landmark speedy-trial + undertrial-rights PIL came from Bihar prisons — H.M. Khatoon's habeas petition exposed 1000s of undertrials; SC declared speedy trial an FR (Art 21) and free legal aid a right — the PIL-and-Bihar link that BPSC can frame either way.
  • BPSC's own home HC: Patna HC hears Bihar service-matter PILs (BPSC appointment litigation is HC-level — a meta-fact).
  • Judicial personalities: Justice B.P. Singh (Patna HC CJ, later SC judge); Bihar's Lal Babu cases on Art 227 superintendence; the state's record in SC appeals (Art 132-136) from Patna HC.
  • Constituent-level link: Ambedkar's Drafting Committee (Topic 38) framed Arts 124-147; Bihar's Anugrah Narayan Sinha & S.K. Sinha lineage (Topic 38/42) shaped the federal-judicial balance Patna HC administers today.
  • Recent Bihar cases: liquor-policy PILs (prohibition litigation — Art 226 challenge), caste-survey litigation (2023 — Patna HC & SC), teacher-appointment disputes (2024-25) — live Art 226/227 illustrations.

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. The Collegium for appointment of Supreme Court judges comprises:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Third Judges Case (1998): SC collegium = CJI + 4 senior-most puisne judges. HC collegium = CJI + 2 senior-most + the concerned HC CJ. NJAC (99th Amdt, 2014) tried replacing it — struck down 2015 (4:1).
Q2. The theory of basic structure of the Constitution was propounded in:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Kesavananda (1973, 7:6): Parliament may amend any part but cannot destroy the basic structure. Golak Nath (1967) held FRs unamendable (later reversed by the 24th Amdt); Minerva Mills (1980) applied basic structure to strike 368(4)(5).
Q3. Under Article 143, the President can seek the Supreme Court's advisory opinion. The opinion is:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 143(1): advisory, not binding on the President; SC may decline. Exception: 143(2) disputes involving pre-constitutional treaties etc. — SC is bound to report (duty, still not binding on the President).
Q4. Which writ is issued to quash an order already passed by a lower court or tribunal?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Certiorari = curative (quashes a passed order); Prohibition = preventive (stops proceedings before completion). Mandamus commands a public duty; habeas produces a detainee.
Q5. Statement I: Article 226 is wider than Article 32. Statement II: Art 226 covers fundamental rights and any other legal rights. Choose:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — 226 is wider (FRs + legal rights, any cause) — that IS why it is wider; 32 is a guaranteed right (Ambedkar's "heart and soul"), FRs only. Note: 32 is part of the basic structure; suspension only under 359.
Q6. A Supreme Court judge can be removed on grounds of:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 124(4): removal = President on an address by both Houses (special majority: majority of total + 2/3 present & voting) for "proved misbehaviour or incapacity". Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968: 100-MP motion → Speaker/Chairman → 3-member committee.
Q7. The age of retirement of a High Court judge is:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — HC judges retire at 62; SC at 65. The 3-year gap is a deliberate juniority signal (HC→SC elevation gains 3 years).
Q8. Public Interest Litigation in India was pioneered by Justices:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Justices Bhagwati & Krishna Iyer relaxed locus standi (S.P. Gupta 1981; Mumbai Kamgar Sabha 1976 line) — PIL for the poor. Hussainara Khatoon (1979) — from Bihar's prisons — proved the model.
Q9. The law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts in India under:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 141: SC's declared law binds all Indian courts (not the SC itself — larger benches may revisit). Art 142 = complete justice; 143 = advisory; 140 = ancillary powers.
Q10. The Patna High Court was established in:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Patna HC established 1916 (Letters Patent, after the 1912 Bihar-Orissa separation created the province; earlier the Calcutta HC's Patna Bench). 2026 = its 110th anniversary year — a BPSC-friendly hook.
Q11. Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979) established:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Bihar prisoners' PIL: speedy trial = FR (Art 21); free legal aid = right; undertrials' release orders followed. A PIL-landmark with a Bihar anchor — BPSC's dream combo.
Q12. The Supreme Court's original jurisdiction under Article 131 covers:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 131: Centre↔state, state↔state disputes (federal jurisdiction) — exclusive to the SC, excluding matters where interim relief existed pre-constitution or which are covered elsewhere (e.g., Finance Commission, election petitions).
Q13. Under Article 136, the Supreme Court may grant special leave to appeal from any judgment/order of any court/tribunal. This is:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 136 SLP = discretionary, exceptional, any court/tribunal (except military tribunals) — wider than any apex court's cert power elsewhere.
Q14. The NJAC Act and the 99th Constitutional Amendment were struck down by the Supreme Court in 2015 because:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — NJAC (CJI + 2 senior judges + Law Minister + 2 eminent persons) was struck down 4:1 (2015): executive voice in appointments compromised judicial independence — part of the basic structure. The Court thereby preserved the collegium.
Q15. District judges in a state are appointed by:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 233: Governor in consultation with the HC appoints district judges (not the PSC for posting/promotion; Shetty 1992 barred executive discretion). Bihar's recent district-judge recruitment litigation (2024-25) tests this.
Q16. Which Article empowers High Courts to issue writs?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 226: HC writs for FRs + legal rights (territorial + cause-based jurisdiction post-15th Amdt). 227 = superintendence (administrative); 32 = SC's FR writs.
Q17. The Supreme Court is a court of record under:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 129: SC = court of record (powers to punish contempt — Art 129(2)); HCs under Art 215. Contempt of Courts Act 1971 (civil + criminal categories).
Q18. Statement I: Habeas corpus literally means 'to have the body of'. Statement II: It can be sought against both public and private detention. Choose:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Habeas = "you may have the body" (produce the detainee); available against private detention too (the only writ against private parties) — but that availability doesn't explain the literal meaning — II is independent.
Q19. The first woman judge of the Supreme Court was:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Justice M. Fathima Beevi (1989) — first woman SC judge. Ruma Pal (2000) much later; Indu Malhotra (2018) was the first woman lawyer directly elevated from the Bar.
Q20. 'Procedure established by law' in Article 21 was transformed into substantive due process in:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Maneka (1978): procedure must be fair, just, reasonable — importing due-process substance into Art 21. ADM Jabalpur (1976) is the discredited detention case (overruled 2017); Gopalan was the narrow original reading.
Q21. The number of Supreme Court judges (including the CJI) currently sanctioned is:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — SC (Number of Judges) Amendment 2019: CJI + 33 = 34 (from 31). Original 1950: 7 + CJI... 8 total; growth 8→11→14→18→26→31→34.
Q22. Art 227 superintendence of High Courts extends over:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 227: HC superintendence over all courts/tribunals except military (session courts, family, labour, etc.); L. Chandra Kumar (1997) restored tribunal judicial review through HCs/SC.
Q23. In the Judges (Inquiry) Act 1968 process, the 3-member inquiry committee comprises:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Committee: SC judge + HC CJ + distinguished jurist (1968 Act). Motion: 100 MPs (LS) / 50 (RS) notice → presiding officer's admission → committee report → both Houses' special-majority address → President's order.
Q24. The electoral bonds scheme was struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2024 primarily violating:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Feb 2024: electoral bonds scheme unconstitutional — voters' right to know (Art 19(1)(a)); the scheme's anonymity + unlimited corporate funding also failed Art 14 proportionality. SBI was directed to disclose; the scheme stands quashed.
Q25. Match the PIL case with its domain: Hussainara (1), Olga Tellis (2), Vishaka (3), MC Mehta (4). Domains: (a) environment, (b) workplace harassment, (c) speedy trial, (d) pavement dwellers' rights:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Hussainara = speedy trial (Bihar, 1979); Olga Tellis = pavement dwellers (1985); Vishaka = workplace harassment guidelines (1997); MC Mehta = environment (Ganga/oleum gas series, 1986-).
