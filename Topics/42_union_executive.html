The President (Arts 52–62) — election, powers, vetoes

  • Art 52: There shall be a President of India (the Union Executive comprises President + VP + PM + CoM + Attorney General).
  • Election (Art 54–55): indirect — electoral college = elected MPs (LS+RS) + elected MLAs of States & Delhi/Puducherry (NOT nominated members, NOT MLCs); proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot; quota = (valid votes)/(electors +1) +1. Value of MLA vote based on the 1971 census (frozen).
  • Impeachment (Art 61): violation of the Constitution; resolution signed by ≥1/4 members with 14 days' notice; passed by 2/3 majority of total membership in each House; no President has ever been impeached.
  • Veto powers: Absolute veto (withhold assent — e.g., PEPSU Appropriation Bill 1954, Hindu Marriage amendments 1991); Suspensive veto (return once — overridden by simple majority; NOT for Money Bills); Pocket veto (sit on the bill indefinitely — Zail Singh 1986 on Indian Post Office Amendment). No qualified veto in India.
  • Ordinance (Art 123): when Parliament not in session (or either House not in session post-RC Cooper reading); must be approved within 6 weeks of reassembly; lapses after 6 months + 6 weeks max; can be withdrawn; judicially reviewable (RC Cooper, DC Wadhwa, Krishna Kumar Singh).
  • Pardoning powers (Art 72): pardon (absolves), commutation, remission, respite, reprieve — for Union-law offences, all martial-law and all death-sentence cases. Governor's Art 161: cannot pardon death sentence (only respite etc. for state-law offences).
  • 42nd vs 44th Amendment: 42nd made President bound by CoM's advice; 44th allowed the President to return advice once for reconsideration (must then accept re-tendered advice).
  • Current: Droupadi Murmu — 15th President (sworn 25 Jul 2022), first tribal President, second woman; from Odisha. Vice-President: Jagdeep Dhankhar (since Aug 2022).
Rashtrapati Bhavan — the President of India is the constitutional head (Arts 52–62): elected indirectly, nominal executive, with real power vested in the CoM headed by the PM (Art 74).

Vice-President (Arts 63–71)

  • Elected by both Houses of Parliament (elected + nominated members) — MLAs play NO role (opposite of presidential election — the classic BPSC contrast).
  • Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Art 64); acts as President when the office falls vacant (max 6 months — Art 65; a new President must be elected within 6 months).
  • Removal: resolution in Rajya Sabha (no formal impeachment) + agreed by LS; 14 days' notice.
  • No salary as VP — draws salary as RS Chairman.

PM & Council of Ministers (Arts 74–78)

  • Art 74: CoM with the PM at the head to aid and advise the President; the 42nd/44th made advice binding (with one reconsideration window).
  • Art 75: PM appointed by the President; ministers appointed on PM's advice; CoM collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Art 75(3) — the pivot of parliamentary government).
  • Art 75(1A): total ministers (incl. PM) ≤ 15% of Lok Sabha strength (91st Amendment 2003).
  • Art 75(1B): disqualification on defection — merged 91st Amendment rules.
  • Art 78: the President's right to information — PM communicates Cabinet decisions etc.
  • Cabinet vs CoM: Cabinet = 30-40 senior ministers (Art 352 definition); CoM = all ministers. 91st Amendment: defector ministers to quit within... (limit 15%, defection bar for defectors).
  • Kitchen Cabinet (inner circle), Shadow Cabinet (opposition's would-be ministers — British concept, not Indian).

Bihar Connection 🔗
  • President Rajendra Prasad (Siwan, Bihar) — the longest-serving President (1950–62, two terms) and the only President from Bihar. BPSC pairs "first President" with his Assembly-President role (Topic 38 link).
  • Bihar's Presidential politics: Bihar's electoral-college votes (MLAs + MPs) matter in presidential contests; the 1969 and later contests saw Bihar's Congress/Socialist MPs play decisive roles.
  • Presidential assent & Bihar bills: the 70th BPSC's Bihar Legislative Council question traces to the Government of India Act 1935 + Bihar Legislative Council Act 1911 (Council created 1911/1912 era — bicameralism; note: Bihar abolished its Council in 2020 via a resolution, awaiting Parliament's nod — a live example of Art 169).
  • Art 356 in Bihar: President's Rule imposed in Bihar multiple times (1967–68, 1969, 1972, 1977, 1980, 1995, 1999, 2005 — 8+ times); the 2005 dissolution (buta) earned SC censure in Rameshwar Prasad (2006) — a landmark Art 356 case from Bihar itself.
  • VPs with Bihar links: while no Bihar-native VP, Rajya Sabha Chairmanship interactions (Bihar MPs' role in VP elections) supply distractor material.
  • PM & Bihar: Bihar-born leaders in Union Cabinets (Jagjivan Ram, Anugrah Narayan Sinha in the Constituent era) illustrate CoM composition; the 1977 first non-Congress PM (Morarji) government had major Bihar ministers (Karpoori Thakur stayed CM).

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. The President of India is elected by:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 54: elected MPs (LS+RS) + elected MLAs of states; the 70th Amendment (1992) added Delhi & Puducherry MLAs. Nominated members and MLCs are excluded — BPSC's standard trap (option C omits MLAs entirely; option D is absurd).
Q2. The Vice-President of India is elected by:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — The VP is elected by all MPs (elected + nominated) of both Houses — the exact opposite of the President's college (no MLAs). This contrast is BPSC's single most repeated executive question.
Q3. An ordinance promulgated by the President ceases to operate at the expiry of:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 123: the ordinance must be laid before Parliament; it lapses 6 weeks from reassembly unless approved — i.e., maximum life = 6 months + 6 weeks from promulgation. Option A is only half the rule (misses the 6-month outer limit); option B omits the reassembly window.
Q4. Which of the following pardoning powers does the Governor NOT possess (Art 161)?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The Governor can commute/remit/respite/reprieve for state-law offences, but cannot pardon a death sentence (only suspend/respite it) — and has no court-martial jurisdiction. The President's Art 72 covers all death sentences + martial law.
Q5. Under Article 75, the total strength of the Council of Ministers (including the PM) cannot exceed:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 75(1A), added by the 91st Amendment (2003): ≤15% of LS strength. The same amendment also disqualifies defectors from ministership. State CoMs have a parallel 15% cap (Art 164(1A)) with a floor of 12 ministers.
Q6. [71st BPSC] The Attorney General of India has the right of audience:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 76(3): the AG enjoys the right of audience in all courts in India (the 71st BPSC statement "not entitled to audience except the Supreme Court" was FALSE). He may also participate in parliamentary proceedings (no vote) and is not a whole-time government servant.
Q7. When the office of President falls vacant, the Vice-President discharges the functions for a maximum period of:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — A presidential election must be held within 6 months of the vacancy (Art 62); the VP acts meanwhile. Historic: V.V. Giri (1969) acted, then resigned to run, and B.D. Jatti acted later — the 1969 episode is BPSC-history material.
Q8. The President can be removed from office on grounds of 'violation of the Constitution' through:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 61 impeachment: charge in either House (≥1/4 members' notice, 14 days), passed by 2/3 of total membership; the other House investigates (President may appear) and votes by the same majority. No President has faced it; no-confidence (A) applies to the CoM, not the President.
Q9. The 'suspensive veto' of the President means:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Suspensive veto: return once (Art 111); overridden by simple majority re-passage — NOT available for Money Bills. Option C is the pocket veto (used by Zail Singh, 1986); option A describes absolute veto; option D has no constitutional basis.
Q10. The value of an MLA's vote in the presidential election is based on the population census of:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 55 uses the 1971 census for the MLA vote value (frozen via the 42nd Amendment's population freeze and extended by later amendments). Formula: State population (1971)/(1,000 × elected MLAs) × (effective LS seats/total elected MLAs)... simplified: each MLA's value = 1971-pop-based; each MP's value is uniform. The freeze is the exam point.
Q11. Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 64: the VP is ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. The President is not a member of either House (Art 72-style separation); the Deputy Chairman (elected by RS) presides in the VP's absence.
Q12. Under Article 78, the President has the right to:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 78 enumerates the PM's duties to communicate: (a) Cabinet decisions, (b) need for parliamentary action on CoM business, (c) requested information — the President's right to information. The 44th Amendment also added the duty to submit reconsidered advice back for the President's action.
Q13. Which one of the following is NOT a pardon-type power?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — The five are pardon, commutation, remission, respite, reprieve — P-C-R-R-R. "Review" (judicial re-examination) is NOT a pardon power. BPSC loves inserting judicial terms into executive lists.
Q14. The 44th Amendment (1978) to Article 74 provided that:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — The 42nd made advice binding; the 44th added the one-time reconsideration window — the re-tendered advice must be accepted. This is the modern "aid and advice" doctrine (post-1978 constitutional position).
Q15. Match List-I with List-II:

(a) Zail Singh (b) V.V. Giri (c) Rajendra Prasad (d) Droupadi Murmu
1) First President 2) First tribal President 3) Pocket veto (Indian Post Office Bill) 4) Acting President who resigned to contest (1969)
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Zail Singh = pocket veto (1986); V.V. Giri = 1969 acting President who resigned and won; Rajendra Prasad = first President (1950–62, from Bihar); Droupadi Murmu = first tribal President (2022–). Mixing first/woman/tribal "firsts" is a standard BPSC matching frame.
Q16. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 75(3): collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha — the bedrock of parliamentary government. A no-confidence motion passes against even one minister's conduct → whole CoM resigns. The Rajya Sabha can criticise but not remove the CoM.
Q17. Consider the statements about the Solicitor General of India:
1. The office is created by the Constitution under Article 76.
2. The Solicitor General assists the Attorney General.
3. Only the AG has the right to speak in Parliament.
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Statement 1 is WRONG: only the AG is constitutional (Art 76); the SG/Additional SGs are non-constitutional (executive) posts. Statement 2 correct (they assist). Statement 3 is misleading — the AG "may take part" in House proceedings; the SG has no parliamentary rights, but the framing "only AG may speak" is accurate; however, combined with 1's error, only statement 2 stands alone as unambiguously correct.
Q18. The President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha on the ground of:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 80(3): special knowledge/practical experience in literature, science, art and social service. BPSC sometimes adds "sports" (option C) as a trap — sports is NOT in Art 80(3). Sports figures have been nominated under the general categories, but the constitutional list is the four fields.
Q19. The pocket veto refers to:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Pocket veto = inaction on a bill (no constitutional time-limit on assent). India's only instance: Zail Singh's 1986 Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill. The US President has a 10-day pocket-veto limit; India has none — making the Indian pocket veto "stronger".
Q20. Under Article 169, a state legislature (Bihar's case) can abolish its Legislative Council if:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 169: state assembly resolution by special majority (not simple — option A's phrasing is the exam convention "by a resolution"; the correct majority is special) followed by a Parliament law (simple majority). Bihar's assembly resolved to abolish its Council in 2020 — pending parliamentary action. Note the majority nuance: special, not simple.
Q21. Which of the following appointments is NOT made by the President?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The Speaker is elected by the Lok Sabha (Art 93), not appointed by the President. The President appoints the CJI/judges, CEC, CAG, UPSC chairman, Governors, Finance Commission etc. (all under various articles).
Q22. The impeachment of the President requires that the resolution:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 61(2): the charge must pass by 2/3 of the total membership of each House (not present-and-voting, no joint sitting). The 14-day notice requires ≥1/4 members' signatures. No President has ever been impeached in India.
Q23. The 'kitchen cabinet' refers to:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — "Kitchen cabinet" = the PM's informal, trusted inner group (often non-ministers) that influences decisions outside formal Cabinet channels. "Shadow cabinet" = the opposition's alternative front-bench (British concept). BPSC tests these labels' meanings.
Q24. In the event of a conflict between the President's pardon (Art 72) and a court-martial sentence, which is true?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 72 expressly covers "all cases where the sentence is a sentence of court-martial" — a President-exclusive domain (Governors have no military jurisdiction). Options B/D create false exclusions; option C invents a CDS power.
Q25. Consider the statements about the Vice-President's removal:
1. Impeachment procedure mirrors the President's.
2. A Rajya Sabha resolution (with LS agreement) removes the VP.
3. 14 days' notice is required.
4. No VP has been removed so far.
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Statement 1 is WRONG: the VP is NOT impeached like the President; Art 67(b) = RS resolution (majority of members then present & voting, agreed by LS simple majority), 14-day notice. No VP has been removed to date — but the 1969 (Giri-era) disputes were near-misses.
