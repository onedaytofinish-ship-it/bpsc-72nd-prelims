B3. Bihar's Separation from Bengal (1905–1936) — Statehood Chronology

Bihar was part of the Bengal Presidency from 1765 (after the Battle of Buxar and the grant of Diwani to the East India Company) until 1912. The demand for a separate province was driven by the neglect of Bihar's interests under Bengal's administration — no separate educational institutions, under-representation in government, and economic exploitation. The movement was led by Sachchidananda Sinha, who argued that Bihar's size, population, and historical importance warranted separate status.

C2. The Tinkathia System — How the Exploitation Worked

  • The system: European indigo planters (mostly British) forced Champaran peasants to cultivate indigo on 3 kathas out of every 20 kathas (15%) of their land — hence "tin-kathia" (tin = three, kathi = a land measure).
  • Coercion: Peasants who refused were beaten, fined, or had their land seized. The planters set the price for indigo, which was far below what the peasants could earn from food crops on the same land.
  • Double exploitation by 1917: German synthetic indigo had made natural indigo unprofitable. The planters kept forcing the system anyway, but now extracted tawan (compensation) from peasants who wanted to stop — a payment for releasing them from the obligation they never wanted in the first place.
  • Other abuses: Fraudulent contracts; illegal enhancement of rent (abwabs = illegal cesses); arbitrary fines; physical intimidation by planters' agents (naibs); forced labour (begar); seizure of cattle and property for non-payment.
  • Why indigo mattered: Indigo dye was a major export crop for the East India Company/British planters. The Champaran-Betiahat belt was one of India's premier indigo-producing regions from the late 18th century.

D3. Bihar Kisan Sabha & the Peasant Movement (1929–1940)

AspectDetail
Founded1929 — Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha
FounderSwami Sahajanand Saraswati (1889–1950) — a sannyasi who renounced his hermitage to lead peasants
AimAbolition of zamindari; land to the tiller; relief from exploitative rent, illegal cesses (abwabs), and begar (forced labour)
Bakasht movement (1930s)Tenants in Darbhanga, Muzaffarpur, and Champaran demanded ownership of bakasht land (land they had tilled themselves for years but which zamindars claimed). Sahajanand led the agitation. The Darbhanga Maharaj was the biggest opponent. The Bihar government passed the Bakasht Land Act (1938) under Kisan Sabha pressure, giving occupancy rights to bakasht tenants.
Relation with CongressInitially aligned with INC; later Sahajanand broke with Congress ministries (1937–39) for failing to abolish zamindari. He opposed Sri Krishna Singh's government from the left.
JP's roleJayaprakash Narayan was associated with the Kisan Sabha as a socialist leader (71st PYQ Q100 pairs Kisan Sabha → JP). JP helped organise peasant unions in Bihar's Saran and Bhojpur districts.
Individual Satyagraha 1940Sahajanand Saraswati offered Individual Satyagraha in Bihar (71st PYQ Q100). Nationally, Vinoba Bhave was the first Individual Satyagrahi (chosen by Gandhi); Nehru was second. Sahajanand was chosen as the Bihar representative.
LegacyThe Kisan Sabha's pressure eventually led to the Zamindari Abolition Act (Bihar, 1950) — one of the first states to abolish zamindari after Independence. Sahajanand is remembered as "Kisan Prasad."
71st PYQ Q100 The matching question pairs Kisan Sabha Bihar → JP Narayan AND Individual Satyagraha 1940 → Sahajanand Saraswati. Note the apparent paradox: Sahajanand founded the Kisan Sabha, but the PYQ pairs the Kisan Sabha with JP (who was associated as a leader, not founder). This is a deliberate trap — memorise the exact PYQ pairing, not the historical nuance.

🔶 Bihar Connection — Deep Dive
Why Bihar Matters in the Freedom Movement
  • First Indian satyagraha: Champaran (1917) was Gandhi's first act of civil disobedience in India — it established the template for all future movements. The method (arrive, survey, document, refuse to leave, negotiate) was born in Bihar.
  • 1857 Bihar martyr: Kunwar Singh (Jagdishpur) was the oldest and one of the most determined rebel leaders — fought across three provinces at age 80.
  • Quit India hotspot: Bihar was among the top three states for Quit India intensity (with Bombay and Bengal). JP's Hazaribagh escape became legendary; the Azad Dasta was a rare organised underground guerrilla unit.
  • Peasant mobilisation: Bihar Kisan Sabha (1929, Sahajanand Saraswati) was one of India's strongest peasant movements — linking the freedom struggle to the land question.
  • Constitutional leadership: Rajendra Prasad (Siwan) — President of the Constituent Assembly and first President of India. Sachchidananda Sinha (Arrah) — first temporary chairman of the Constituent Assembly.
  • First BJP-era CM: Sri Krishna Singh (1946–61) — Bihar's longest-serving CM, "Bihar Kesari". He was the first elected head of Bihar under provincial autonomy.
  • Tribal resistance: Santhal Hool (1855–56) — Sidho and Kanhu Murmu's rebellion was one of India's earliest tribal uprisings against colonial-zamindari oppression, predating 1857.
  • Pre-1947 INC session: The 1912 Bankipore (Patna) session was the only INC session in Bihar before Independence — hosted by Sachchidananda Sinha.

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. Kunwar Singh, the prominent leader of the 1857 Revolt in Bihar, was the zamindar of which place?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Kunwar Singh was the 80-year-old zamindar of Jagdishpur (Ara, Bhojpur district, Bihar). The 70th BPSC Q127 tested this exact matching (Kunwar Singh → Jagdishpur). Patna was the administrative centre, Munger the arsenal, Bhagalpur the old Anga port — none were Kunwar Singh's base.
Q2. Kunwar Singh died on which date, mortally wounded in his final battle?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Kunwar Singh died on 26 April 1858 at Jagdishpur, mortally wounded in his final battle. He cut off his wounded hand and offered it to the Ganga. Option B (17 June 1858) is the death date of Rani Lakshmibai at Gwalior. Option C (8 April 1857) is Mangal Pandey's hanging. Option A (18 April 1859) is Tantia Tope's execution.
Q3. The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 was launched by Mahatma Gandhi against which system?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — The tinkathia system forced Champaran peasants to cultivate indigo on 3/20 of their land for European planters at below-market rates. Gandhi investigated, recorded 8,000 statements, and the Champaran Agrarian Act 1918 abolished the system. Option B is the general zamindari issue (not Champaran-specific). Option C is the 1930 Salt Satyagraha. Option D is the 1918 Kheda Satyagraha (Gujarat).
Q4. [71st BPSC] Match the following Bihar events with their leaders and choose the correct code:

List I: (a) Champaran Satyagrah (b) Azad Dasta (c) Individual Satyagraha in Bihar 1940 (d) Kisan Sabha Bihar
List II: 1) Jayaprakash Narayan 2) Shyam Narayan Singh 3) Sahajanand Saraswati 4) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — (a) Champaran → 4) Rajendra Prasad; (b) Azad Dasta → 2) Shyam Narayan Singh; (c) Individual Satyagraha 1940 → 3) Sahajanand Saraswati; (d) Kisan Sabha → 1) JP Narayan. This is the exact 71st BPSC Q100. Note: Gandhi led Champaran but Rajendra Prasad was the Bihar-specific associate tested. JP is linked to Kisan Sabha, not Azad Dasta — a common confusion.
Q5. [71st BPSC] The Champaran Agrarian Inquiry Committee was appointed in agreement between:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The committee was formed in 1917 as a bilateral agreement between the Indian Government (colonial administration) and Gandhiji personally. It had 4 members including Gandhi. Its report led to the Champaran Agrarian Act (1918). Option B is wrong because the planters were the accused party, not co-signers. Option C is wrong because it was not an INC-government deal. Option A is wrong because the Kisan Sabha was founded later (1929).
Q6. Who became the Chief Minister of Bihar in March 1939? [71st BPSC Q86]
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Shri Krishna Singh ("Bihar Kesari") became Premier/CM of Bihar in March 1939. He had first become Premier in 1937 after INC won elections under the GoI Act 1935, resigned Oct 1939 over WWII, and returned as CM in 1946 (serving until 1961). Option A (JP) was a socialist leader, never CM. Option B (Rajendra Prasad) became President of India, not Bihar CM. Option D (Anugrah Narayan Sinha) was Deputy CM, not CM.
Q7. The Santhal Hool (Rebellion) took place in which years? [70th BPSC Q108]
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The Santhal Hool took place in 1855–56, led by Sidho and Kanhu Murmu in the Rajmahal hills (Bhagalpur-Birbhum). It pre-dates the 1857 Revolt by two years. Option B (1857–58) is the Great Revolt. Option C (1895–1900) is roughly the Birsa Munda revolt period. Option A (1831–32) is the Kol uprising. "Hool" means rebellion in Santhali.
Q8. When was the Bihar State separated from Bengal? [70th BPSC Q62]
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — The 70th BPSC accepted 1911 (Delhi Durbar announcement by George V on 12 Dec 1911). The formal province of Bihar & Orissa was constituted on 1 Apr 1912. BPSC has historically accepted 1911. Option A (1905) is the Bengal Partition year. Option C (1915) is incorrect. Option D (1936) is when Orissa was separated from Bihar (making Bihar a standalone province).
Q9. Who gave the slogan "Do or Die" and during which movement? [70th BPSC Q20]
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Gandhi gave the "Do or Die" (Karenge ya Marenge) slogan at the Bombay INC session on 8 Aug 1942 (Gowalia Tank Maidan, now August Kranti Maidan) during the Quit India Movement. Bihar was a hotspot for this movement. Option A: Bose gave "Give me blood, I shall give you freedom." Option B: Tilak gave "Swaraj is my birthright." Option D: Bhagat Singh's slogan was "Inquilab Zindabad."
Q10. Who led the Azad Dasta during the Quit India Movement in Bihar?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Shyam Narayan Singh led the Azad Dasta, the underground guerrilla group in Bihar during Quit India 1942. They sabotaged railways, cut telegraph wires, and attacked police stations. This is the 71st PYQ Q100 pairing. Option A (JP) organised the underground but the Azad Dasta was specifically led by Shyam Narayan Singh — a common confusion. Option C (Sri Krishna Singh) participated but did not lead the Azad Dasta. Option D (Sahajanand) was associated with Individual Satyagraha 1940, not Azad Dasta.
Q11. Who invited Mahatma Gandhi to visit Champaran in 1917?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Raj Kumar Shukla, a local indigo cultivator from Champaran, met Gandhi at the Lucknow INC session (1916) and persuaded him to visit Champaran. Gandhi arrived on 10 April 1917. Options B, C and D all joined Gandhi at Champaran later, but it was Shukla who first invited him. This is a BPSC-pattern detail question testing the specific individual behind the invitation.
Q12. Jayaprakash Narayan escaped from which jail during the Quit India Movement in November 1942?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — JP escaped from Hazaribagh Central Jail in November 1942 (Hazaribagh was then in Bihar, now in Jharkhand). He went underground, organised the Bihar resistance, and was arrested in September 1943. The escape became one of the legendary events of Quit India. The other jails listed were not associated with JP's escape.
Q13. Who was the first President of the Constituent Assembly of India, and from which district of Bihar did he hail?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Rajendra Prasad, born in Zeradei (Siwan district, Bihar), was the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly (from 11 Dec 1946). He later became the first President of India (1950–62). Note: Sachchidananda Sinha (from Arrah) was the first temporary chairman on 9 Dec 1946, but Rajendra Prasad was the elected permanent President. Option B confuses the temporary chairman role with the permanent presidency. Option C gets the district wrong (Siwan, not Patna).
Q14. The Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha was founded in which year and by whom?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Sahajanand Saraswati founded the Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha in 1929, mobilising peasants against zamindari exploitation. He later offered Individual Satyagraha in Bihar in 1940 (71st PYQ Q100 pairing: Kisan Sabha → JP is the test answer, but the Kisan Sabha itself was founded by Sahajanand). Note: the 71st PYQ paired "Kisan Sabha Bihar → JP Narayan" — JP was associated with the Kisan Sabha movement as a leader, while Sahajanand was the founder. Option B (1920) is the Non-Cooperation year. Option C (1936) is the Bihar separate province year. Option A (1942) is Quit India.
Q15. The only INC session held in Bihar before 1947 was at which location and in which year?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — The 1912 Bankipore (Patna) session was the only INC session held in Bihar before Independence in 1947. It was hosted by Sachchidananda Sinha (temporary president; Rahimtula M. Sayani was the elected president). Option B (1934 Patna) is incorrect — the 1934 session was in Bombay (Rajendra Prasad presided). Option C (Gaya 1922) is incorrect — the 1922 session was in Gaya but it was NOT the one commonly tested; the Bankipore 1912 session is the canonical answer. Option D is fabricated.
Q16. Who was the Deputy Premier (later Deputy CM) of Bihar under Sri Krishna Singh?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Anugrah Narayan Sinha was Deputy Premier (1937) and later Deputy CM (1946) under Sri Krishna Singh. He was a Gandhi follower from the Champaran era and was arrested during Quit India 1942. Option A (JP) was a socialist leader, never in the Bihar cabinet. Option C (Sahajanand) was a peasant leader who opposed the Congress ministry on land reform. Option D (Sachchidananda Sinha) was the Constituent Assembly temporary chairman, not Deputy CM.
Q17. Bihar Diwas is celebrated annually on which date?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Bihar Diwas is celebrated on 22 March, commemorating the formation of the province of Bihar & Orissa. The 2026 celebration was the 114th Bihar Diwas with the theme "Pragatishil Bihar, Ujjwal Bihar." Option A (1 April) is the date the province was formally constituted (1912), but the commemorative day is 22 March. Option C (12 December) is the Delhi Durbar date. Option B (15 August) is Independence Day.
Q18. Consider the following statements about Kunwar Singh:
(1) He was 80 years old during the 1857 Revolt
(2) He was the zamindar of Jagdishpur in Bhojpur district
(3) He fought in Bihar, UP and MP during the revolt
(4) His brother Amar Singh continued the fight after his death
Which of the above are correct?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — All four statements are correct. (1) Kunwar Singh was born in 1777 and was 80 in 1857. (2) He was the zamindar of Jagdishpur (Ara, Bhojpur district, Bihar). (3) He fought in Bihar (Ara, Danapur, Patna), UP (relieved Azamgarh), and MP (Rewa, Bundelkhand). (4) His brother Amar Singh continued the fight after Kunwar Singh's death on 26 Apr 1858, until captured and executed in 1859.
Q19. Who was the first temporary chairman of the Constituent Assembly of India, and from where in Bihar did he come?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Sachchidananda Sinha, from Arrah (Bihar), was the first temporary chairman of the Constituent Assembly on 9 Dec 1946. The next day (11 Dec 1946), Rajendra Prasad was elected as the permanent President. Sinha was also the key advocate for Bihar's separation from Bengal (1912) and hosted the 1912 Bankipore INC session. Option A confuses the temporary chairman with the permanent President. Options C and D are not from Bihar.
Q20. The Santhal Hool of 1855–56 was led by which pair of brothers?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Sidho and Kanhu Murmu led the Santhal Hool (1855–56) in the Rajmahal hills (Bhagalpur-Birbhum area), rebelling against zamindari oppression and moneylenders. Option B: Birsa Munda led a later tribal revolt (1895–1900) in Chhotanagpur. Option C: Kunwar and Amar Singh were the 1857 leaders, not Santhal. Option D: Tilka Manjhi led an earlier Santhal uprising (1784) against the British. The "Hool" is commemorated annually as Hool Diwas (30 June).
Q21. Which of the following movements was NOT associated with Bihar during the freedom struggle?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — The Dandi March (12 Mar–6 Apr 1930) took place from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi (Gujarat coast), led by Gandhi. It was NOT a Bihar event. Bihar had its own salt satyagraha on the banks of the Ganga at Patna (option B). Champaran (A) and Individual Satyagraha by Sahajanand (D) were Bihar-specific events. The question asks which was NOT associated with Bihar.
Q22. Arrange the following Bihar-related events in chronological order:
(1) Champaran Satyagraha (2) Santhal Hool (3) Bihar separated from Bengal (4) Quit India Movement
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Correct chronological order: (2) Santhal Hool (1855–56) → (3) Bihar separated from Bengal (1911–12) → (1) Champaran Satyagraha (1917) → (4) Quit India Movement (1942). Option B is wrong because Bihar separation (1911) came before Champaran (1917). Option C is wrong because Santhal Hool (1855) pre-dates Bihar separation (1911). Option D starts with Champaran, which is the third event, not the first.
Q23. "Bihar Kesari" was the title given to which Bihar leader?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Shri Krishna Singh was given the title "Bihar Kesari" (Lion of Bihar) for his role in the freedom movement and his long tenure as CM (1946–61). The 69th BPSC Q134 specifically noted this title. Option A (Rajendra Prasad) was known as "Rajendra Babu" or "Desh Ratna." Option B (JP) was known as "Lok Nayak" (People's Leader). Option D (Sahajanand Saraswati) was known as "Kisan Prasad" or simply "Swamiji."
Q24. Consider the following about the 1937 Bihar elections:
(1) INC won the elections under the Government of India Act 1935
(2) Sri Krishna Singh became the first Premier of Bihar
(3) Anugrah Narayan Sinha became Deputy Premier
(4) The ministry resigned in October 1939 over WWII
How many of the above are correct?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — All four are correct. (1) The 1937 elections were held under the GoI Act 1935 (provincial autonomy), and INC won Bihar. (2) Sri Krishna Singh became first Premier. (3) Anugrah Narayan Sinha became Deputy Premier. (4) The INC ministry resigned in October 1939 because the Viceroy declared war on India's behalf without consulting elected ministries. All four are historically accurate.
Q25. Which of the following is NOT correctly matched regarding Bihar's freedom movement leaders and their titles/roles?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Sahajanand Saraswati was NOT called "Bihar Kesari" — that title belongs to Shri Krishna Singh (option A). Sahajanand Saraswati was a peasant leader known as "Kisan Prasad" or simply "Swamiji." Option A is correctly matched. Option B is correct — JP was "Lok Nayak" (People's Leader), especially after the 1974 movement. Option C is correct — Rajendra Prasad was "Desh Ratna" (Jewel of the Nation). The question asks which is NOT correctly matched.
