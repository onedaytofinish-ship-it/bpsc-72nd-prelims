TOPIC #101 • TIER B
Buddhism and Jainism — Bihar: Bodh Gaya, Vaishali, Pawapuri
📂 Ancient History ⚖️ Weight: 0.7 🎯 Expected: 2–3 Qs 🔶 Bihar: Bodh Gaya (enlightenment), Vaishali (Mahavira birth), Pawapuri (Mahavira death), Nalanda, Buddhist Councils
📚 Core Content

The most heavily examined block in ancient Indian history — and for BPSC its geography is almost entirely Bihari. The Buddha was born in Nepal and died in UP, but he became the Buddha in Bihar; Mahavira was born, enlightened and liberated within a hundred kilometres of Patna.

1. Why the Sixth Century BCE Exploded

The sramana (renouncer) movement erupted in the middle Ganga plain because that region was passing through the Second Urbanisation (c. 600–300 BCE). Iron ploughshares cleared the damp forest of eastern UP and Bihar, paddy transplantation raised yields, and a storable agrarian surplus built towns — Rajagriha, Champa, Vaishali, Kaushambi, Shravasti. Its signature is NBPW pottery, burnt brick and the silver punch-marked karshapana.

  • New wealth, old rank. The gahapati (landowning householder) and setthi (merchant-banker, head of a shreni) were rich but ranked Vaishya — wealth without status breeds heterodoxy.
  • Ritual expense. Later Vedic sacrifice was priest-heavy and costly; the new faiths offered salvation at no sacrificial cost.
  • Cattle sacrifice hurt agriculture. Yajnas destroyed draught animals needed for the plough, making ahimsa economically rational as well as ethical.
  • Usury. The Dharmasutras treated moneylending as impure; neither new faith condemned trade or interest.
  • Kshatriya assertion. Both founders were Kshatriya, and both ranked Kshatriya above Brahmana.
  • Language. The Vedas were closed Sanskrit; the Buddha used Pali, Mahavira Ardhamagadhi.
EXAM TIP Causation questions are standard. If asked for the economic cause of the new religions, answer with the iron-based agrarian surplus and the rising trading class, not "opposition to caste" alone. R.S. Sharma's chain — iron, surplus, towns, coins, gahapati and setthi — is the expected framing.

The Ajivikas matter for Bihar: Ashoka dedicated the Barabar caves (Jehanabad) to them, Dasharatha the Nagarjuni caves — India's oldest rock-cut caves, and Ajivika, not Buddhist.

3. The Buddha and the Ashtamahasthanas

Siddhartha of the Gautama gotra was born at Lumbini to Suddhodana of the Shakya republic and Mahamaya, and raised by his aunt Mahaprajapati Gautami; his wife was Yashodhara, his son Rahul. The four sights prompted the Mahabhinishkramana at twenty-nine. He studied under Alara Kalama and Uddaka Ramaputta, abandoned austerity after accepting milk-rice from Sujata at Uruvela, and was enlightened beside the Niranjana (Phalgu). Uruvela is the old name of Bodh Gaya — expect that substitution. Dates: traditionally 563–483 BCE, alternatively c. 480–400 BCE. Pilgrimage recognises eight great places: four chief events, four miracles; three are in Bihar.

5. Pratityasamutpada, Anicca, Anatta, Madhyama Pratipada

  • Pratityasamutpada (dependent origination) — nothing exists independently. Twelve nidanas: ignorance → mental formations → consciousness → name-and-form → six sense-bases → contact → feeling → craving → clinging → becoming → birth → old age and death. Break a link and rebirth stops.
  • Anicca — everything conditioned is impermanent; tradition gives this as the Buddha's dying instruction.
  • Anatta — no permanent soul; the person is five skandhas (form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness). The sharp break from Brahmanical atman and Jain jiva.
  • Madhyama pratipada — the Middle Path between indulgence and mortification; where Buddhism parts from Jain austerity.
  • Karma as intention — for the Buddha karma is volition, for Jainism matter. This contrast answers many comparison questions.
  • Silence on God — metaphysics set aside as unhelpful; Buddhism is anishvaravadi, non-theistic rather than atheist.

6. Sangha and Tripitaka

The Sangha admitted all castes, and women after Ananda interceded for Mahaprajapati Gautami at Vaishali. Its four limbs are bhikkhus, bhikkhunis, upasakas and upasikas; laity keep the five precepts (panchashila), monks the Patimokkha.

PitakaContentsNotes

Key non-canonical works: Milindapanha (Menander and Nagasena), the Dipavamsa, Mahavamsa and Ashvaghosha's Buddhacharita.

9. Mahavira and the Tirthankara Line

Jainism claims no founder. Mahavira is the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of the present cycle, a reformer of an older faith. The first was Rishabhanatha (Adinatha), emblem the bull, described in the Vishnu and Bhagavata Puranas as an incarnation of Narayana; the twenty-second, Arishtanemi, is held to be a cousin of Krishna. Only the last two are generally treated as historical. Parshvanatha, the twenty-third — prince of Kashi, son of Ashvasena, emblem the serpent, c. eighth century BCE — taught four restraints; Mahavira added a fifth.

VowMeaningParshvanathaMahavira
AhimsaNon-injuryYesYes
SatyaTruthfulnessYesYes
AsteyaNot taking what is not givenYesYes
AparigrahaNon-possessionYesYes
BrahmacharyaCelibacyAbsentAdded

Parshva's followers kept an upper garment; Mahavira required nudity of ascetics — a difference that later fed the schism. Vardhamana was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali to Siddhartha of the Jnatrika clan and Trishala, sister of the Licchavi chief Chetaka; he married Yashoda and had a daughter, Priyadarshana. Renouncing at thirty, he wandered twelve years — six with Makkhali Gosala — and won kevala jnana at forty-two under a sal tree by the Rijupalika at Jrimbhikagrama near Rajgir. Thereafter Jina, Mahavira, Kevalin and Nirgrantha, he taught thirty years through eleven Ganadharas (only Sudharman outlived him) and died at Pava aged seventy-two. Dates: traditionally 599–527 BCE, alternatively c. 540–468 BCE.

🧠 The vow Mahavira added: Parshvanatha gave A-Sa-A-A — Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha. Mahavira supplied the missing B: Brahmacharya.
"Parshva forgot the B." If asked which vow Mahavira added, the answer is always celibacy — never ahimsa or aparigraha.

10. Jain Doctrine — Jewels, Anekantavada, Syadvada, Sallekhana

  • Ratnatraya — samyak darshana (faith), samyak jnana (knowledge), samyak charitra (conduct); all three together, never singly, give moksha.
  • Mahavrata and anuvrata — the five vows absolutely for ascetics, diluted for householders. This graded ethic kept a large lay following without softening monastic rigour.
  • Jiva and ajiva — souls are granted even to earth, water, fire, air and plants, so Jain ahimsa reaches far beyond animals.
  • Karma as matter — karmic particles physically adhere to the soul. Seven tattvas: jiva, ajiva, asrava (inflow), bandha, samvara (stoppage), nirjara (shedding by austerity), moksha; adding punya and papa gives nine.
  • Anekantavada — reality is many-sided and no standpoint captures it all; the blind men and the elephant.
  • Syadvada — its logical form: every statement holds only conditionally, prefixed by syat. Formalised as saptabhangi, sevenfold predication (it is; it is not; it is and is not; it is inexpressible; and three combinations).
  • Sallekhana (santhara) — voluntary fasting to death when the body can no longer sustain religious life; relinquishment, not suicide. The Rajasthan High Court held it illegal in 2015; the Supreme Court stayed that order.
  • No creator God — the universe is eternal, cycling through utsarpini and avasarpini; liberated souls rest on the siddhashila.

The split traces to a twelve-year Magadhan famine. Bhadrabahu led monks south to Shravanabelagola, accompanied — tradition says — by Chandragupta Maurya, who died there by sallekhana. Sthulabhadra stayed in Magadha, allowed white cloth and convened the Pataliputra council; the returning group rejected both robes and canon.

Jain literature is mainly in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit: 12 Angas (the Acharanga oldest, the Bhagavati richest on Mahavira and Gosala), 12 Upangas, 10 Prakirnas, 6 Chhedasutras, 4 Mulasutras, 2 Chulikasutras. The Kalpasutra, ascribed to Bhadrabahu, narrates the Tirthankaras' lives and is recited at Paryushana. Umasvati's Tattvarthasutra is accepted by both sects and Kundakunda's Samayasara is the Digambara classic. Jain writers also enriched Tamil, Kannada and Apabhramsa.

Mahavir Mandir, Patna. Bihar holds Mahavira's whole biography — birth at Kundagrama, omniscience near Rajgir, nirvana at Pawapuri — plus the First Jain Council at Pataliputra. Trap: the second Jain council was at Valabhi in Gujarat, so never generalise that all councils were Bihari.

Spread and decline. Ashoka's missions carried Buddhism to Sri Lanka, Kashmir-Gandhara and Suvarnabhumi; the Kushanas took it along the Silk Road to China. In India it faded for converging reasons: the shift from Pali to Sanskrit distanced it from ordinary speech; land grants made monasteries wealthy and discipline slack; image worship made it resemble the Brahmanism it had opposed, and the Buddha was absorbed as an avatara of Vishnu; Shankaracharya and the Bhakti saints drew away lay support; Vajrayana's esoteric turn alienated householders; patronage collapsed after the Palas; and around 1200 CE Bakhtiyar Khalji destroyed Nalanda, Vikramashila and Odantapuri — a monastery-centred faith could not outlive its monasteries. Jainism, resting on a lay merchant base, survived.

14. Traps Worth Memorising

  • Uruvela = Bodh Gaya; Rajagriha = Rajgir; Pataliputra = Patna; Champa = near Bhagalpur.
  • The Buddha's tree is the pipal; Mahavira's kevala jnana came under a sal.
  • The Barabar caves are Ajivika, dedicated by Ashoka — neither Buddhist nor Jain.
  • Ananda recited the Sutta, Upali the Vinaya — do not reverse them.
  • Kathavatthu belongs to the Third Council; Mahavibhasha to the Fourth.
  • Bhadrabahu went south, Sthulabhadra stayed in Magadha — reversing them is the commonest error here.
  • The Jatakas and Dhammapada lie inside the Khuddaka Nikaya, not separate pitakas.
  • UNESCO years — Nalanda 2016, Mahabodhi 2002, Lumbini 1997.
  • Numbers: 11 Ganadharas; 12 nidanas, 12 Angas; 24 Tirthankaras; 45 Agamas; 62 views; 547 Jatakas; 7 tattvas.
TRAP ALERT The "everything happened in Bihar" overreach. Examiners target the student who has learned Bihar as the cradle of both faiths. The exceptions: the Buddha's birth (Lumbini), first sermon (Sarnath), death (Kushinagar) and the Fourth Council (Kashmir); on the Jain side the Second Council (Valabhi) and the art centres of Shravanabelagola, Mount Abu and Palitana.
🔶 Bihar Connection
Bihar — the ground on which both religions were made
  • Bodh Gaya (Gaya district). Ancient Uruvela on the Niranjana (Phalgu); Sujata's offering at Bakraur; enlightenment under the pipal, then seven weeks at seven spots. The Mahabodhi Temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2002), run under the Bodh Gaya Temple Act, 1949.
  • Rajgir (Nalanda district). Ancient Rajagriha, first Magadhan capital, ringed by five hills. Bimbisara gave the Veluvana; the Buddha preached from Gridhrakuta; the First Buddhist Council met in the Saptaparni cave under Ajatashatru.
  • Rajgir is equally Jain. Mahavira won kevala jnana at nearby Jrimbhikagrama and spent about fourteen rainy seasons in Rajgir and Nalanda — more than anywhere else.
  • Vaishali (Vaishali district). Mahavira was born at Kundagrama, a suburb of Vaishali, to the Jnatrika clan; his mother Trishala was a Licchavi princess. For Buddhism: Ambapali gifted her mango grove, the order of nuns began with Mahaprajapati Gautami's ordination, the Buddha gave his last sermon and announced his coming parinirvana here, and the Second Buddhist Council met under Kalashoka. The Kolhua pillar carries an intact lion capital.
  • Pawapuri (Nalanda district). Mahavira's nirvana at seventy-two; the Jal Mandir, a marble temple in a lotus tank, marks the cremation site; tradition links the lamps lit that night to the festival of lights.
  • Pataliputra / Patna. The Third Buddhist Council under Ashoka (c. 250 BCE), presided over by Moggaliputta Tissa, launched the missions abroad; the First Jain Council under Sthulabhadra also met here — one city, both canons.
  • Nalanda. Founded by Kumaragupta I in the fifth century CE; Asia's greatest Mahayana university, endowed by Harsha and the Palas, described by Xuanzang (pupil of Shilabhadra) and Yijing; sacked c. 1200 CE; UNESCO 2016.
  • Vikramashila (Antichak, Bhagalpur) and Odantapuri (Biharsharif, Nalanda). Pala foundations and homes of Vajrayana; Atisha Dipankara, who carried Buddhism to Tibet, was abbot at Vikramashila. The name Bihar comes from vihara.
  • Champa (Bhagalpur district). Capital of Anga and an old Jain centre; tradition places the birth and nirvana of the twelfth Tirthankara Vasupujya at Champapuri.
  • Kesariya (East Champaran) and Barabar–Nagarjuni (Jehanabad). Kesariya's colossal stupa recalls the Buddha's gift of his alms bowl to the Licchavis; Barabar and Nagarjuni are India's oldest rock-cut caves, and Ajivika.
  • The birthplace dispute. Shvetambaras and the state government identify Kundagrama with Basokund near Vaishali; Digambaras point to Kundalpur near Nalanda. For BPSC, write Kundagrama, Vaishali.
BIHAR FOCUS The sharpest distinction here: the Buddha's birth and death fall outside Bihar — Lumbini in Nepal, Kushinagar in UP — while nearly everything between happened inside it: enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, the teaching years at Rajgir, the last sermon at Vaishali, the first three councils. For Mahavira the pattern reverses: birth, omniscience and death are all Bihari.
🔗 Cross-Topic Connections
  • Topic 100 — Mahajanapadas: the political frame. The Buddha was a Shakya, Mahavira a Jnatrika of the Vajji confederacy; both taught in Magadha, Kosala and Vatsa. Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Prasenajit and Udayin are shared characters, and the urbanisation that built the mahajanapadas produced the sramana movement.
  • Topic 102 — Mauryan Empire: Ashoka's Third Council and his missions turned a regional sect into a world religion; his edicts are the earliest datable evidence of Buddhist patronage. Jain tradition remembers Chandragupta Maurya as Bhadrabahu's disciple, and Ashoka and Dasharatha endowed the Ajivika caves at Barabar and Nagarjuni.
  • Topic 106 — Art & Literature: stupas at Sanchi, Bharhut, Amaravati and Kesariya; the rock-cut chaityas and viharas of Karle, Bhaja, Ajanta and Ellora; Gandhara and Mathura Buddha images; Jain caves at Udayagiri–Khandagiri and temples at Mount Abu and Palitana. The Tripitaka, Jatakas and Agamas are India's earliest non-Vedic literature.
  • Topic 139 — Bhakti & Sufi Movements: the medieval saints repeated the sixth-century BCE pattern — rejection of ritual and priestly monopoly, regional languages, openness to all castes and to women. Their emotional theism, with Shankaracharya's Advaita, helped displace Buddhism by absorbing its lay following.
  • Topic 155 — Ancient Bihar: (topic not yet built) the direct continuation — Vaishali's early republic, Rajagriha and Pataliputra as successive capitals, Nalanda and Vikramashila as monastic universities, Bodh Gaya and Pawapuri as living pilgrimage economies, and the state's name from vihara.
📰 Current Relevance
  • Bodh Gaya: the most important Buddhist pilgrimage site in the world — the Mahabodhi Temple is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2002). Dalai Lama visits regularly. International Buddhist conferences held here.
  • Nalanda: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016). A new Nalanda University has been established near the ruins (2014) — as an international university, reviving the ancient legacy.
  • Pawapuri: a major Jain pilgrimage site — the Jal Mandir attracts thousands of Jain pilgrims every year, especially during Mahavir Jayanti.
  • Buddhism globally: ~500 million Buddhists worldwide (mostly in China, Japan, SE Asia, Sri Lanka, Tibet). Buddhism spread from Bihar (via Ashoka's missionaries, 250 BCE) to become a world religion.
  • Jainism globally: ~4-5 million Jains (mostly in India — Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Karnataka). Jainism's principle of ahimsa influenced Gandhi and the Indian freedom movement.

✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Tripitaka = Buddhism. Three baskets: Sutta Pitaka (discourses), Vinaya Pitaka (monastic rules), Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophy). In Pali. Compiled at First Council (483 BCE).
Q11. The sacred texts of Jainism are called:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Agamas (Jainism). 12 Angas + 12 Upangas + others. In Ardhamagadhi Prakrit. Svetambaras accept 45 Agamas; Digambaras believe original texts were lost.
Q12. The principle of 'Ahimsa' (non-violence) is most strictly followed in which religion?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Jainism = strictest ahimsa. Monks wear masks (avoid inhaling insects), sweep ground (avoid stepping), filter water (avoid microorganisms), avoid root vegetables. Buddhism allows meat (if not killed for the monk).
Q13. How many Tirthankaras are there in Jainism?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — 24 Tirthankaras. First: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha). 23rd: Parshvanatha (c. 8th century BCE). 24th: Mahavira (c. 599-527 BCE). Not founders — they rediscover and teach Jainism in each cosmic cycle.
Q14. The Buddhist Sangha (monastic community) consisted of:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 4 groups: monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen. Open to all — regardless of caste, gender, social status. Revolutionary in caste-ridden Vedic society. Governed by Vinaya Pitaka.
Q15. The Five Precepts (Pancha Sila) of Buddhism are:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 5 precepts: no killing, no stealing, no sexual misconduct, no lying, no intoxication. For lay followers. Monks follow additional Vinaya rules (227 for monks, 311 for nuns).
Q16. Buddhism split into two main branches at the Fourth Buddhist Council (c. 72 CE). These are:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Hinayana (Theravada, 'Way of the Elders' — individual liberation, Pali, Sri Lanka/SE Asia) vs Mahayana ('Greater Vehicle' — Bodhisattva, Sanskrit, East Asia). Split at Fourth Council (Kashmir, c. 72 CE, Kanishka).
Q17. Jainism split into two main sects after Mahavira's death. These are:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Digambara (sky-clad, no clothes, South India) vs Svetambara (white-clad, white robes, West India). Digambaras: women cannot achieve moksha. Svetambaras: women can. Different Agamas.
Q18. The three jewels (Triratna) of Buddhism are:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Triratna: Buddha (teacher), Dharma (teaching), Sangha (community). 'I take refuge in the Buddha, Dharma, Sangha.' Core declaration of Buddhist faith.
Q19. The three jewels (Triratna) of Jainism are:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Jain Triratna: Right faith (Samyak Darshana), Right knowledge (Samyak Jnana), Right conduct (Samyak Charitra). All three together = path to moksha.
Q20. The Five Vows (Pancha Mahavrata) of Jainism are:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — 5 vows: ahimsa (non-violence), satya (truth), asteya (non-stealing), brahmacharya (celibacy), aparigraha (non-possession). Stricter for monks (Mahavrata); less strict for lay (Anuvrata). Aparigraha uniquely Jain.
Q21. Bihar's connection with Buddhism includes which of the following sites?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Bihar = cradle of Buddhism: Bodh Gaya (enlightenment), Rajgir (First Council), Vaishali (Second Council + last sermon), Pataliputra (Third Council + Ashoka), Nalanda (university). All first 3 Councils in Bihar!
Q22. Bihar's connection with Jainism includes which of the following sites?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Bihar = cradle of Jainism: Vaishali/Kundagrama (Mahavira birth, c. 599 BCE), Pawapuri (Mahavira death, c. 527 BCE), Rajgir (Kevala Jnana + first Jain council, Pataliputra).
Q23. Nalanda University, the great Buddhist centre of learning, was located in:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Nalanda (Bihar, near Rajgir). Founded by Kumargupta I (c. 427 CE). 10,000 students, 2,000 teachers. Subjects: Buddhism, logic, medicine, astronomy. Destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khalji (~1200 CE). UNESCO (2016).
Q24. Which Buddhist council was held at Pataliputra (Bihar) under Ashoka?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Third Council: Pataliputra (Bihar, c. 250 BCE), Ashoka. Presided by Moggaliputta Tissa. Compiled Abhidhamma Pitaka. Sent Buddhist missionaries abroad — Buddhism became a world religion.
Q25. The Buddha and Mahavira were contemporaries. Which statement about them is correct?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Both preached in Magadha-Kosala (Bihar + UP). Contemporaries (Buddha 563-483 BCE, Mahavira 599-527 BCE). Both: rejected Vedas + caste; used vernacular; emphasised ahimsa; attracted merchants. 'Sramana movement' — reaction against Vedic Brahmanism. Primarily in modern Bihar and UP.
