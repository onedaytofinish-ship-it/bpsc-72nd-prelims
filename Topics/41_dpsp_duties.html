DPSP & Fundamental Duties — Articles 36–51 and Part IVA
📂 Indian Polity ⚖️ Weight: 0.9 🎯 Expected: 1–2 Qs 📅 Exam: 26 Jul 2026
📚 Core Content & PYQ Alignment
69th BPSC PYQ 42nd Amendment's additions (69th, Q104-class): BPSC tested that the 42nd Amendment (1976) added new Directive Principles — Articles 43A and 48A (worker participation; environment) — alongside Fundamental Duties (Art 51A). Expect a direct "which DPSPs were added by which amendment" or "Fundamental Duties were added by..." question.
BPSC Pattern DPSP classification traps: BPSC repeatedly tests the three-way classification — Socialist (Arts 38, 39, 39A, 41, 42, 43, 43A), Gandhian (Arts 40, 43, 46, 47, 48), Liberal-Intellectual (Arts 44, 45, 48A, 49, 50, 51) — and the DPSP enforcement cases (Champakam Dorairajan, Minerva Mills). Memorise which Articles sit in which bucket.

DPSP — nature and characteristics

  • Part IV, Arts 36–51 — "Directives" to the State (Art 12 definition of "State" applies); borrowed from the Irish Constitution (which took it from Spain).
  • Non-justiciable (Art 37: not enforceable, yet "fundamental in the governance of the country" — State's duty to apply them in law-making).
  • Distinct from FRs: FRs are political/social democracy tools, justiciable; DPSPs are economic democracy goals, non-justiciable.
  • K.T. Shah called Part IV "a veritable dustbin of sentiments"; Ambedkar: DPSPs are "instrument of instructions" — but the SC balances both (Minerva Mills: FRs & DPSPs are "conscience" + complementary).
  • DPSPs seek to establish a "welfare state" (Madhya Hoshiary Samiti; D.S. Nakara: socialism = welfare state).

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar — he described Part IV as an "instrument of instructions"; DPSPs are non-justiciable but "fundamental in governance" (Art 37).

DPSP case-law ladder

Champakam Dorairajan (1951): FRs prevail over DPSPs if conflict; DPSPs must run subsidiary to FRs → led to 1st Amendment
Kesavananda (1973) + 42nd Amdt's Art 31C: DPSPs 39(b)/(c) protected from Art 14/19 challenge
Minerva Mills (1980): Art 31C's blanket protection struck down — balance restored: FRs and DPSPs are complementary, neither superior; "harmonious construction"

Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art 51A) — the 11 duties

  • Added by 42nd Amendment (1976) on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee; originally 10 duties; the 11th (education of children 6–14 by parents/guardians) added by the 86th Amendment (2002).
  • Applicable to citizens only (not foreigners); non-justiciable — no direct enforcement, but courts use them to interpret laws.
  • Japan is the only country with FD-type provisions in its Constitution (USSR inspiration via Swaran Singh).

Bihar Connection 🔗
  • Art 39(d) & equal pay: Bihar's large informal workforce (agriculture, brick-kilns) is the classic illustration for equal-pay DPSP enforcement debates.
  • Art 40 in Bihar's soil: the Bihar Panchayati Raj Act (1993 post-73rd) operationalised Art 40; Bihar was among the earliest states to hold panchayat elections after the amendment. Karpoori Thakur's 1978 panchayat push preceded it.
  • Art 47 & prohibition: Bihar is India's flagship prohibition state (since 2016) — a direct Gandhian-DPSP implementation story BPSC loves as a scenario. Art 47 exhorts prohibition of intoxicants "except medicinal".
  • Art 46: Bihar's post-1947 focus on SC/ST educational schemes (residential schools, stipends) and its caste-census-driven policy debates (Topic 21) track Art 46.
  • Art 48A & Bihar ecology: Valmiki Tiger Reserve, Ganga dolphin (national aquatic animal) protection, and flood-plain management illustrate environmental DPSP.
  • FD (e) & Bihar: the JP movement (1974) and Bihar's composite culture (Sufi-Buddhist-Jain heritage) supply examples of harmony/composite-culture duties.
Cross-Topic Connections: Preamble (justice ideals ↔ DPSP) → 39; FRs & harmonious construction (Minerva) → 40; 42nd Amendment's full impact → 49; Panchayats (Art 40 → 73rd) → 48; Bihar panchayats/prohibition → 144, 15; Environment (48A) → 150–154.
🆕 Current Relevance 2024–26
  • UCC debate (Art 44): Uttarakhand enacted a UCC (2024) — the first post-Independence state UCC — reigniting the Art 44 debate; SC observations on personal-law reform keep it examinable.
  • DPSP & welfare economics: SC's 2024–25 rulings on welfare-scheme access (food security, pensions) cite Arts 39/41; "living wage" (Art 43) code implementation is a live NITI/labor-ministry theme.
  • Art 48A in courts: climate-rights jurisprudence (MC Mehta line → 2024 "right against adverse climate change" recognition in RK Hardash Singh case) extends the environmental DPSP.
  • Fundamental Duties revival: SC/HC judgments 2024–25 cite FDs (g), (h), (i) — e.g., tree-felling, vandalism cases; NCERT/curriculum pushes on duties education.
  • Bihar angle: Bihar's prohibition litigation (2024–25 HC rulings on seizure rules) is a live Art 47 scenario; its caste-survey-based schemes are Art 46 in action.

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. The Directive Principles of State Policy were borrowed from the Constitution of:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — DPSPs come from the Irish Constitution (which itself drew from Spain's 1937 Constitution). Britain gave parliamentary government; USA gave FRs/judicial review; USSR gave FDs & the justice ideal.
Q2. Article 37 states that the Directive Principles are:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 37: DPSPs are not enforceable by courts, BUT are "fundamental in the governance of the country" — the State's duty to apply them in law-making. Option D understates their constitutional status; option C was the 42nd's transient claim, struck in Minerva Mills.
Q3. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution on the recommendation of:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — The Swaran Singh Committee (1976) recommended FDs → added by the 42nd Amendment. The Verma Committee (1999) later identified laws to implement them (option A is the classic confusion). Sarkaria = Centre-State; Ashok Mehta = panchayats.
Q4. Which of the following Directive Principles were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976)?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — The 42nd added 39A (legal aid), 43A (worker participation), 48A (environment) and recast 38/39. Option C's 43B (co-operatives) came with the 97th Amendment (2011) — the trap BPSC most loves.
Q5. The 11th Fundamental Duty (Art 51A(k)) — duty of parents/guardians to provide education opportunities to children aged 6–14 — was added by:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — The 86th Amendment (2002) added Art 51A(k) alongside Art 21A and the recast Art 45 (early childhood). The 42nd added the original 10 duties; 97th is co-operatives.
Q6. Which one of the following is a Gandhian Directive Principle?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 47 (prohibition, nutrition, public health) is Gandhian, along with 40 (panchayats), 43 (cottage/living wage), 43B, 46, 48. Arts 44, 50, 51 are liberal-intellectual. Bihar's prohibition law is the live example.
Q7. Fundamental Duties are applicable to:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — FDs bind citizens only ("It shall be the duty of every citizen of India"). They are not addressed to the State (unlike DPSPs) nor to foreigners (unlike some FRs like Arts 14/21).
Q8. In Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980), the Supreme Court:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Minerva Mills struck the 42nd's Art 31C extension (which had shielded all DPSPs from Arts 14/19): FRs & DPSPs are complementary "conscience" elements — neither is superior (harmonious construction). Only 39(b)/(c) retain 31C protection (Kesavananda's reading).
Q9. The Directive Principle of "equal pay for equal work" is found in:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 39(d): equal pay for equal work for both men and women — implemented via the Equal Remuneration Act 1976. Art 41 is right to work/education; Art 43 is living wage; Art 47 is nutrition/prohibition.
Q10. Consider the statements about Fundamental Duties:
1. Originally there were 10 duties; the 11th was added in 2002.
2. They are justiciable in courts of law.
3. Japan's Constitution also contains duties of citizens.
4. The Verma Committee (1999) examined their implementation.
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 1 correct (10 + (k) in 2002); 2 WRONG (non-justiciable); 3 correct (Japan — the only other modern constitution with such duties); 4 correct (Verma Committee identified implementation laws).
Q11. Which Article directs the State to promote co-operative societies?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 43B (added by the 97th Amendment, 2011) directs promotion of co-operative societies — the newest Gandhian DPSP. 43A is worker participation (42nd); 43 is living wage/cottage industries; 46 is SC/ST education.
Q12. The phrase 'instrument of instructions' (used for DPSPs) was used by:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Ambedkar described Part IV as a veritable "instrument of instructions" (drawn from the GoI Act 1935's Instructions). K.T. Shah (option C) derided it as a "dustbin of sentiments"; Austin praised the FR-DPSP pairing as the "Conscience of the Constitution".
Q13. In Champakam Dorairajan (1951), the Supreme Court held that:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Champakam Dorairajan (1951): FRs prevail; DPSPs run subsidiary. This ruling triggered the 1st Amendment (Art 15(4)). The balance shifted via 25th/42nd's Art 31C and settled in Minerva Mills (1980) as harmonious construction.
Q14. Article 45, after the 86th Amendment, now directs the State to provide:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — The 86th Amendment (2002) MOVED 6–14 education INTO Art 21A (justiciable) and RECAST Art 45 for early childhood care & education below 6 years. Option A is the OLD Art 45 text (now Art 21A) — a classic post-2002 trap.
Q15. The Uniform Civil Code directive is contained in:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 44: the State shall endeavour to secure a UCC for all citizens — a liberal-intellectual DPSP. Uttarakhand's 2024 UCC law is the first state implementation. 43 = living wage; 47 = prohibition; 48 = agriculture/cattle.
Q16. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Voting is a statutory/legal right, NOT a Fundamental Duty. (B), (C), (D) are duties 51A(h), (i), (j). BPSC's "NOT a duty" list usually includes voting, paying taxes, obeying laws — none of which are FDs.
Q17. Match List-I (Directive) with List-II (Article):

(a) Village panchayats (b) Free legal aid (c) Environment protection (d) International peace
1) Art 48A 2) Art 40 3) Art 51 4) Art 39A
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Panchayats → 40; legal aid → 39A; environment → 48A; international peace → 51. The four most-tested "new-ish" DPSP articles in one matching frame.
Q18. The 97th Amendment (2011) is related to:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — 97th (2011): co-operative societies — inserted Art 43B (DPSP) and Part IXB; made right to form co-operatives an FR (Art 19(1)(c) + 19(4) extension). 52nd = anti-defection; 86th = education; 103rd = EWS.
Q19. Which of the following pairs is/are correctly matched? (Fundamental Duty — Clause)
1. Protect natural environment — 51A(g)
2. Develop scientific temper — 51A(h)
3. Safeguard public property — 51A(i)
4. Provide education opportunities to children — 51A(k)
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — All four are correctly paired: (g) environment & compassion for creatures; (h) scientific temper; (i) public property, abjure violence; (k) education duty (86th Amdt). Memorise letter-tag pairs — BPSC tests exact clauses.
Q20. "The Directive Principles are like a cheque payable at the convenience of the bank" — this criticism reflects their:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — The cheque-on-demand criticism (K.T. Shah's "cheque on a bank payable only when able") targets non-justiciability: lofty goals without court enforcement. (T.S. Rau's coinage is related.) Note: this differs from Cripps' "post-dated cheque" for the Mission plan — distinct quote traps.
Q21. Which Directive Principle was described by Dr. Ambedkar as the "soul of the Constitution"?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Ambedkar said Art 40 (village panchayats) is the "soul of the Constitution" — a favourite one-liner. He called Art 32 the heart-and-soul of the Constitution (option C mixes the two quotes — and Art 32 isn't even a DPSP).
Q22. Consider the statements about Article 48:
1. It directs organisation of agriculture and animal husbandry on modern scientific lines.
2. It prohibits slaughter of cows and calves.
3. It extends the ban to all cattle whatsoever.
4. Milch and draught cattle are specifically mentioned.
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 48 bans slaughter of cows, calves and other milch & draught cattle — NOT "all cattle whatsoever" (statement 3 overreaches; the Constitution doesn't mandate a total beef ban; State laws vary). Statements 1, 2, 4 are accurate.
Q23. The right to free legal aid (Art 39A) has been read by the Supreme Court into which Fundamental Right?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Hussainara Khatoon (1979) & Khatri (1981): free legal aid is implicit in Art 21 (fair trial) and Art 14 (equal access). Legal Services Authorities Act 1987 + NALSA implement Art 39A. A prime example of DPSP-FR fusion.
Q24. Which of the following correctly pairs a Directive Principle with its implementing legislation?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — All three pairings are correct: 39A ↔ NALSA/Legal Services Act; 39(d) ↔ Equal Remuneration Act; 40 ↔ 73rd Amendment (Panchayats). DPSP-implementation matching is a recurrent BPSC format.
Q25. Fundamental Duties differ from Fundamental Rights in that duties are:
1. owed by citizens towards the State/society, not claims against the State
2. non-justiciable
3. contained in Part IVA (Art 51A)
4. enforceable via writs
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — 1, 2, 3 correct: duties are citizen-obligations, non-justiciable, Part IVA/51A. 4 is wrong — writs (Art 32/226) enforce FRs, not duties. Courts use FDs only interpretively (e.g., AIIMS Students' Union).
