Federalism & Centre-State Relations — Lists, Governor, Finance, ISC
📂 Indian Polity ⚖️ Weight: 0.9 🎯 Expected: 2 Qs 📅 Exam: 26 Jul 2026
📚 Core Content & PYQ Alignment
71st BPSC PYQ Concurrent List subjects (71st, Q59): BPSC gave a subject set and asked which belongs to the Concurrent List — recall: education, forests, marriage, criminal law, trade unions, electricity are Concurrent; police, public order, health, agriculture = State; defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency, railways = Union. Also 71st Q62 (schedules matching — allocation of seats in councils of state = 4th Schedule; forms of oath = 3rd) and 70th Q76-77 (Nagaland-type Schedule questions — 5th/6th Schedule distribution).
BPSC Pattern Union-with-an-unitary-tilt: BPSC repeatedly frames India as "quasi-federal" (KC Wheare) / "federal in form, unitary in spirit" (Jennings) / "cooperative federalism" (modern SC idiom). Key anchors: Art 1 "Union of States" (not federation), 7th Schedule's three lists, Art 249/312 RS resolutions, Governor's dual role, Art 356, Finance Commission (Art 280), GST Council (101st Amdt), NITI Aayog's cooperative-competitive blend.

Seven Constitutional Amendments that changed federalism

  • 1st (1951): added 9th Schedule — state land-reform laws' protection (Bihar's zamindari-abolition laws were the trigger!) — I.R. Coelho (2007) later opened pre-1973 laws to review.
  • 7th (1956): States Reorganisation — linguistic states; the federation's first big reshaping.
  • 42nd (1976): education/forests moved State→Concurrent; 5 subjects to Union (a centralising jolt).
  • 73rd/74th (1992): third tier — constitutional status for panchayats/municipalities (11th/12th Schedules).
  • 101st (2016): GST — "one nation one tax"; GST Council (Art 279A): Union 1/3 vote weight, states 2/3, decisions by 3/4 — a new federal chamber.
  • FRBM-era compacts + 15th FC: vertical share 41% (2021-26), horizontal devolution criteria — demographic performance, income distance, area, forest — fiscal-federalism's modern maths.
State Emblem (Ashoka Lion Capital) — the Union's seal; India's "Union of States" (Art 1) carries an indestructible-union DNA with federal features.

The Governor — the federal flashpoint

  • Appointment (Art 155): by the President (Centre's nominee); tenure 5 years, serves at President's pleasure (Art 156) — no impeachment.
  • Discretion (Art 163): reservation of bills (Arts 200-201), CM appointment in hung houses, dismissal on lost majority, President's rule report (Art 356) — B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010): Governor's removal not reviewable per se, but arbitrariness is.
  • Bihar milestone: Rameshwar Prasad (2006) — the SC struck down the 2005 Bihar Assembly dissolution (Governor Buta Singh's recommendation) — the locus classicus on Governor's misuse; S.R. Bommai (1994) — 356's judicial review + floor-test rule.
  • Reported reforms: Sarkaria (appointed by non-partisan process, eminent person; remove by impeachment-like process) → Punchhi (fixed tenure) — the "convention" debates.

Bihar Connection 🔗
  • Bihar = 1912 first linguistic-province demand's success: the 1912 separation from Bengal pre-dated the linguistic-states era — Bihar was carved on linguistic-cultural grounds (Maithili-Bhojpuri-Magahi identity), making it the pioneer case BPSC loves for "states reorganisation before 1956".
  • 1st Amendment's Bihar trigger: the 9th Schedule was created to shield Bihar Land Reforms and other zamindari-abolition acts (1950-51) — the state's legislation changed Indian federalism's constitutional design.
  • Rameshwar Prasad (2006): Bihar Assembly dissolution case — the SC's strongest check on Governors' abuse (Buta Singh episode).
  • Fiscal federalism's biggest gainer: Bihar gets ~10% of the divisible pool (the largest single-state share); its "special state" demand + the 15th FC's demographic-performance criterion debate (Bihar's young population) are live exam hooks.
  • Special-category status demand: post-2000 bifurcation (Jharkhand took mineral revenue), Bihar's special-category campaign — NITI-era "special package" (₹1.25 lakh crore 2015, more in 2023-25) — cooperative-federalism case study.
  • Inter-state migration: Bihar's migrant workers (Delhi, Maharashtra, Punjab...) — Art 301-307 free-trade + cooperative-policing questions.
  • NITI Aayog & Bihar: aspirational-district programme's Bihar cohort; SDG India Index's Bihar climb (2023-24) — competitive federalism metrics.
Bihar in the Union — one of 28 states in the "indestructible union of destructible states" (Art 1-3); the state receives ~10% of the divisible pool, the fiscal heart of India's cooperative federalism.

✍️ MCQ Practice Set — 25 Questions (BPSC Level)
Q1. [71st BPSC-style] Which of the following subjects is in the Concurrent List?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Education moved State→Concurrent by the 42nd Amendment (1976) (with forests, weights & measures, wildlife protection, administration of justice). Police/public order = State; foreign affairs = Union.
Q2. India is described as 'quasi-federal' primarily because:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Wheare's "quasi-federal": dual polity + single Constitution/citizenship, indestructible union of destructible states, Emergency's unitary conversion, Governor as Centre's agent, Union-dominated amendment. Not a confederation — states can't secede.
Q3. The Governor of a state is appointed by:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 155: the President appoints Governors (in practice the Union Cabinet's choice); tenure 5 years at the President's pleasure (Art 156) — the federal flashpoint Sarkaria/Punchhi sought to soften.
Q4. In the case where the SC struck down the dissolution of the Bihar Legislative Assembly (2005) on the Governor's mala fide recommendation, the case is:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Rameshwar Prasad (2006): the Governor's report recommending Bihar's Assembly dissolution was mala fide — dissolved sine die unconstitutional; floor-test principle reiterated (Bommai's child). Bihar's gift to federal jurisprudence.
Q5. Allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha to states is provided in the:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — 4th Schedule = RS seat allocation (Bihar 16) — 71st BPSC Q62 tested this matching. 2nd = salaries/emoluments; 3rd = forms of oath; 5th = scheduled areas.
Q6. The Finance Commission is constituted under:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Art 280: FC every 5 years (or earlier); 15th FC (N.K. Singh) awarded 41% devolution (2021-26); 16th FC (Arvind Panagariya) from 2025 for 2026-31. Art 281 merely says the President lays FC recommendations before Parliament.
Q7. In the GST Council, the voting weightage of the Centre and states is:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Art 279A: Union 1/3 of total votes, states collectively 2/3; decisions need 3/4 weighted votes — the states hold aggregate veto power. SC (Mohit Minerals, 2022): recommendations aren't binding legislation per se.
Q8. Residuary powers in India are vested in:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 248 + Union List Entry 97: Union holds residuary powers (unlike USA/Australia where states do). Cyber law's early days exemplified Union reach via Entry 97.
Q9. The Inter-State Council was established in 1990 on the recommendation of:
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Sarkaria Commission (1988 report) → ISC via Art 263 (Presidential Order 1990). Punchhi (2010) refined Centre-state relations; Rajamannar (1969, Tamil Nadu) wanted ISC-like bodies earlier.
Q10. Under Art 3, a bill reorganising a state requires:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 3: Parliament's law (simple majority) on the President's recommendation; the affected state legislature's views are not binding — "indestructible union of destructible states" (contrast the USA's indestructible states).
Q11. The 9th Schedule was added by the 1st Amendment (1951) largely to protect:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Bihar Land Reforms litigation triggered the 1st Amendment's 9th Schedule (land-reform immunity). I.R. Coelho (2007): post-1973 9th-Schedule laws are judicially reviewable against basic structure.
Q12. Which pair is correctly matched? (Allocation of seats in the councils of states; forms of oath; anti-defection; languages)
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 71st Q62 tested exactly this: RS allocation = 4th, oath = 3rd, anti-defection = 10th, languages = 8th. (2nd = salaries; 5th/6th = scheduled areas/tribes.)
Q13. Inter-state river water disputes are resolved by tribunals under:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Art 262: Parliament may exclude SC/other jurisdiction over inter-state water disputes — the 1956 Act's tribunals (Cauvery, Krishna, Mahadayi); the 2019 amendment creates a single permanent tribunal. Bihar angle: Kosi/Gandak pacts with Nepal are international, not inter-state.
Q14. The Sarkaria Commission's key recommendation on Governors was:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Sarkaria (1988): eminent non-partisan persons, active politicians avoided, fixed tenure conventions, removal via due process. Punchhi added security of tenure recommendations. Neither implemented in practice.
Q15. 'Cooperative federalism' as a judicial idiom was most famously invoked in:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Bommai (1994): federalism = basic structure; Art 356 judicially reviewable; floor tests mandatory; "cooperative federalism" as the governing spirit — the case that tamed President's rule.
Q16. Which Schedule distributes powers over 'Forests'?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Forests: State List → Concurrent (42nd Amdt 1976) (with education, weights & measures, wildlife, justice administration, population control).
Q17. The 15th Finance Commission's vertical devolution to states was:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — 15th FC (N.K. Singh): 41% vertical devolution (from 42% after J&K's reorganisation). Horizontal criteria: income distance 45%, population 15%, area 15%, demographic performance 12.5%, forest ecology 10% — Bihar ranks first on income distance, making it a top gainer.
Q18. Zonal Councils were created by:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Zonal Councils = statutory (States Reorganisation Act 1956): 5 zones; chaired by the Union Home Minister; Bihar sits in the Eastern Zone (with WB, Odisha, Jharkhand + Sikkim's 2017 addition). ISC (Art 263) is constitutional — the classic distinction.
Q19. Under Article 200, when a state bill is presented to the Governor, he may:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Art 200: the Governor may assent, withhold, or reserve for the President (and may return a non-Money bill once for reconsideration). Under Art 201, the President may assent, withhold, or direct return. The 2024-25 SC timelines on "deemed assent" for pending bills stem from here.
Q20. Kerala's suit against the Centre over state borrowing limits (2023) was filed under:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Kerala's fiscal-autonomy suit (borrowing-limit restrictions; net-borrowing ceiling) invoked Art 131 — the modern Centre-state litigation frontier; SC entertained the dispute — cooperative federalism's stress-test in 2024-25 news.
Q21. The phrase 'Union of States' in Article 1 conveys:
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Ambedkar: "Union" chosen over "Federation" — no agreement, no secession; Art 3 makes states destructible while the Union endures. BPSC's most repeated polity-one-liner.
Q22. Which state's Legislative Assembly dissolution case is the landmark on Governors' mala fide reports?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Bihar 2005 (Rameshwar Prasad): Governor Buta Singh's report mala fide → dissolution unconstitutional. Karnataka 1989 fed Bommai; UP/MP cases are later applications.
Q23. The 42nd Amendment (1976) transferred which subjects from the State to the Concurrent List?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — 42nd Amdt: education, forests, weights & measures, protection of wild animals/birds, administration of justice, population control & family planning → Concurrent. Five subjects also moved to Union List.
Q24. Bihar's share in the central divisible pool under the 15th Finance Commission is approximately:
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Bihar receives ~10% (10.06%) of the devolution — the largest per the horizontal formula (highest income distance). UP ~18% leads in absolute terms; Bihar's ~10% is second-tier — the "fiscal federalism favours Bihar" line.
Q25. The Punchhi Commission (2010) on Centre-state relations recommended:
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Punchhi (2010): Governor's fixed tenure; Art 356 as a last resort after floor test; commonwealth of inter-state commerce reforms; ISC activation — the Sarkaria-plus package.
