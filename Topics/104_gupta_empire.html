TOPIC #104 • TIER B
Gupta Empire — Golden Age — PATALIPUTRA
📂 Ancient History ⚖️ Weight: 0.7 🎯 Expected: 2–3 Qs 🔶 Bihar: Pataliputra (capital), Nalanda University, Gupta heartland
📚 Core Content
💡 Deep-dive ready: Gupta rulers, the Golden Age debate, science and administration in depth, with exam predictions: Open the master detail page →
Exam Core The spine: The Guptas ruled c. 319–550 CE from Pataliputra (Patna, Bihar). Srigupta and Ghatotkacha held only the title Maharaja; Chandragupta I took Maharajadhiraja and the Gupta Era begins 319–320 CE. Samudragupta expanded it (Prayag Prashasti of Harisena); Chandragupta II Vikramaditya destroyed the Western Kshatrapas and hosted Fa-Hien; Kumaragupta I founded Nalanda; Skandagupta repulsed the Hunas and repaired the Sudarshana lake. "Golden Age" describes elite culture and is now heavily qualified — BPSC tests the critique, not only the praise.

A. The Source Base — What Each Source Proves, and Its Blind Spot

For the Mauryas we have edicts and a foreign ambassador. For the Guptas the evidence is court eulogy, coinage and copper-plate charters — material produced by and for the winners. That bias is itself examinable.

B. The Shadowy Founders — Srigupta and Ghatotkacha

  • Srigupta (c. late 3rd century) is named in later Gupta records with the modest, feudatory-grade title Maharaja. He founded the dynasty.
  • Ghatotkacha, his son, likewise only Maharaja. Neither issued coins; no inscription of their own reigns survives.
  • Exam framing: dynasty founder = Srigupta; empire founder / "real founder" = Chandragupta I. Options deliberately blur the two.

C. Chandragupta I — The Lichchhavi Marriage and the Era

  • Assumed Maharajadhiraja — the formal jump from chief to emperor.
  • Married Kumaradevi, a Lichchhavi princess of Vaishali (Bihar), borrowing the prestige of the old republican clan.
  • The King-and-Queen gold type shows both figures and carries the reverse legend Lichchhavayah. Samudragupta styled himself Lichchhavi-dauhitra, "daughter's son of the Lichchhavis".
  • Gupta Era: 319–320 CE. Caution: many numismatists hold that the King-Queen coins were struck by Samudragupta in his father's memory. The epoch is secure; the issuer is not.

D. Samudragupta — Reading the Prayag Prashasti Correctly

Harisena, who held the offices of sandhivigrahika, kumaramatya and mahadandanayaka, composed the eulogy. Its most examined feature is that it sorts subdued powers into distinct categories, each treated differently.

  • He revived the ashvamedha and struck a special Ashvamedha coin type; the prashasti also calls him Kaviraja.
  • The Lyrist (veena) coin type shows him seated playing the veena — the standard "cultured conqueror" image.
  • V.A. Smith's "Napoleon of India" is colonial-era and now judged overblown: he annexed nothing in the south, and the only source could not record a reverse. Learn it as a label given by Smith, and be ready for the criticism.
🧠 Mnemonic — "9 Up, 12 Back": Nine kings of Aryavarta were uprooted (annexed and kept). Twelve of Dakshinapatha were given back (defeated, released, made tributary).
North absorbed, South restored — swap the numbers and you lose the mark.
Mehrauli iron pillar, Qutub complex, Delhi — about 7.2 m and roughly 6 tonnes of wrought iron, still essentially rust-free after some 1,600 years; the resistance comes from the iron's high phosphorus content, which forms a protective passive film. Trap: the inscription names the king only as "Chandra" and calls the pillar a banner of Vishnu — the identification with Chandragupta II is inference. It stands in a Sultanate complex but is not a Sultanate monument.

E. Chandragupta II Vikramaditya — The Western Conquest and Its Economics

  • Destroyed the Western Kshatrapas (Shakas) of Gujarat–Malwa, the last of them Rudrasimha III, around the opening of the 5th century. Titles: Vikramaditya, Shakari, Paramabhagavata.
  • The prize was the western seaboard — Bharuch, Sopara, Kalyan — and the Arabian Sea trade. Ujjain became a western secondary capital; Pataliputra stayed the imperial seat.
  • He adopted the Shakas' silver coinage for the new provinces, restruck with Gupta types. That is why Gupta silver exists at all — inheritance, not invention.
  • Matrimonial diplomacy: married the Naga princess Kuberanaga; married their daughter Prabhavatigupta to the Vakataka Rudrasena II, after whose death she ruled the Deccan as regent.
  • The Udayagiri cave inscription near Vidisha records his minister Virasena and places the king personally on campaign in the west.
  • Fa-Hien travelled in his reign: light taxation on crown land, movement without passes, capital punishment avoided, vegetarianism among the respectable classes — and the segregation of Chandalas, who struck a piece of wood on entering a town so others could avoid them.
  • Navaratna caution: the nine-gems tradition comes from late texts such as the Jyotirvidabharana and is chronologically impossible as a group — Varahamihira belongs to the 6th century. Treat it as tradition; an option calling it "a later, unverified tradition" is the correct one.

F. Kumaragupta I and Skandagupta — Nalanda, and the First Crisis

  • Kumaragupta I (Mahendraditya) had the longest reign. His Bilsad inscription is the earliest dated record of the reign; the Mandsaur inscription of his time records a guild of silk weavers that migrated from Lata to Dashapura and funded a sun temple — the best evidence for Gupta guilds.
  • He founded the Nalanda Mahavihara (Nalanda district, Bihar). Xuanzang names the founder Shakraditya, identified with Kumaragupta I; his seal and coins have been excavated at the site.
  • Late in the reign came the Pushyamitra threat and the first Huna pressure on the north-west.
  • Skandagupta succeeded after a disputed accession and repulsed the Hunas; the Bhitari pillar's image is of the earth shaking when he met them. Titles Vikramaditya and Kramaditya.
  • His Junagadh inscription records repair of the Sudarshana lake after a flood — a ready-made four-dynasty question: excavated under Chandragupta Maurya (Pushyagupta), conduits under Ashoka (Tushaspha), repaired by Rudradaman I, repaired again under Skandagupta.
  • The cost shows in the coinage: later gold pieces are heavier in weight but lower in gold purity. Debasement is the numismatic signature of decline.

L. Science and Learning

  • Aryabhata (born 476 CE) composed the Aryabhatiya in 499 CE, aged 23, at Kusumapura — the old name of Pataliputra, placing the founding text of Indian mathematics in Bihar.
  • Contributions: a place-value system in which position carries value, the precondition for zero; the rotation of the earth on its axis explaining the apparent daily motion of the stars; eclipses caused by shadows, not by Rahu and Ketu; a table of sine (jya) differences; and π = 62832/20000 = 3.1416, which he himself called asanna, "approximate".
  • Varahamihira (6th century): the Brihat Samhita, an encyclopaedia of astronomy, architecture, gemmology, agriculture and omens; the Panchasiddhantika, a digest of five earlier schools including the Romaka and Paulisha siddhantas — proof of Greek–Indian exchange; and the Brihat Jataka.
  • Brahmagupta is 7th century (Brahmasphutasiddhanta, 628 CE) and gave the first systematic rules for zero and negative numbers. He is post-Gupta — a favourite chronology trap.
  • Medicine: the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita (surgery, rhinoplasty, cataract couching) belong to older traditions but were redacted and commented on now. Say "compiled", not "written in the Gupta age".

  • Religion: the rulers were Parama-bhagavatas — Vaishnavas, with Garuda on coins and seals. Image worship and the Puranic pantheon displaced sacrifice as the mainstream, while Buddhism and Jainism kept receiving endowments.
  • Temples: the free-standing structural temple begins here with the earliest Nagara features. Sequence: flat-roofed shrine on a plinth (Sanchi Temple 17, Tigawa) → shrine with low shikhara and sculpted panels (Dashavatara temple, Deogarh) → tall brick shikhara (Bhitargaon, the finest surviving Gupta brick temple).
  • Sculpture: the Sarnath and Mathura schools give the classic Gupta Buddha — clinging transparent drapery, downcast meditative eyes, ornate halo.
Ajanta murals, Maharashtra (UNESCO 1983) — Jataka narratives in tempera on a lime-plastered mud-and-fibre ground, not true fresco. Trap: the finest 5th-century caves (1, 2, 16, 17, 19) were funded under Vakataka patronage, so "built by the Guptas" is wrong; and the earliest caves (9, 10, 12, 13) are Satavahana-era, 2nd century BCE. Use "Gupta period" for the date and "Vakataka" for the patron.

🔶 Bihar Connection
Bihar as the Imperial Core — Not a Footnote
  • Pataliputra (Patna district), the imperial capital: the Guptas ruled from the city of the Mauryas and Shungas; Kumrahar and Bulandi Bagh show Gupta levels directly above Mauryan ones. This is the last time Bihar is the political centre of the subcontinent — power then shifts to Kanauj and finally to Delhi.
  • Fa-Hien on Pataliputra: he stayed about three years studying Sanskrit and copying texts, described the surviving Mauryan palace as work seemingly made by spirits, the annual image-procession on decorated carts, and — most quoted — the free hospitals and charitable dispensaries endowed by wealthy householders, where the poor, orphaned, crippled and diseased got food and medicine without charge. The earliest description of organised charitable medical care in India is set in Patna.
  • Nalanda Mahavihara (Nalanda district), founded by Kumaragupta I: the greatest monastic university of the ancient world, grown over centuries into a complex of viharas, chaityas and libraries. Trap: the Palas were later patrons, not founders; Xuanzang names the founder Shakraditya, identified with Kumaragupta I, and Kumaragupta's seal and coins were excavated on site. Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016. The modern Nalanda University at Rajgir (2014) is a revival, not a continuation.
  • Vaishali (Vaishali district) and Gupta legitimacy: the marriage to the Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi tied the dynasty to Vaishali's famous clan, and Samudragupta advertised the descent as Lichchhavi-dauhitra. Excavations at Basarh produced a large hoard of Gupta-period clay sealings naming officials and guilds — a working administrative office, headquarters of the province of Tirabhukti, ancestor of the name Tirhut.
  • Bodh Gaya (Gaya district): Fa-Hien visited the Bodhi tree and the enlightenment sites. The Mahabodhi Temple's tall pyramidal tower is essentially of Gupta-period form (5th–6th century), restored repeatedly down to the 19th century. UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2002.
  • The Sultanganj Buddha (Bhagalpur district): a colossal copper-alloy standing Buddha over two metres tall, found at Sultanganj on the Ganga in 1861 during railway construction. It is the largest surviving metal image of the Gupta age and shows advanced hollow-casting. Removed to England, it stands today in the Birmingham Museum and Art Gallery — a fixture of repatriation debate and of objective papers.
  • Aryabhata at Kusumapura: the Aryabhatiya of 499 CE was composed at Kusumapura, generally identified with Pataliputra. Bihar can therefore claim the text in which the earth's rotation, the shadow theory of eclipses and a refined value of π first appear together.
  • Rajgir and the Magadha belt: Gupta-period sculpture, terracottas and brick structures occur across Rajgir, Nalanda, Gaya and Patna districts, and the Nalanda metal workshops set the model later followed by Pala bronzes.
  • The afterlife — the Later Guptas of Magadha: a separate dynasty ruled Magadha after the imperial line faded, recorded in the Aphsad inscription (Nawada district) of Adityasena and the Deo Barnark inscription (Rohtas district). They are a different family from the imperial Guptas — a distinction BPSC has the vocabulary to test.

📰 Current Relevance
  • Nalanda University: a new Nalanda University was established (2014) near the ancient ruins — as an international university, reviving the ancient legacy. UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016). A symbol of India's educational heritage.
  • Aryabhata's legacy: India's first satellite was named 'Aryabhata' (1975). Aryabhata's calculation of pi and his heliocentric insights are celebrated as foundational to Indian mathematics and astronomy.
  • Kalidasa's Shakuntala: translated into English by William Jones (1789) — it created a sensation in Europe. Goethe admired it. Kalidasa is often called the 'Shakespeare of India.'
  • Iron Pillar of Delhi: a testament to Gupta-era metallurgy — it has not rusted in ~1600 years. It is a major tourist attraction at Mehrauli (Delhi) and a subject of scientific research.
  • Ajanta Caves: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1983). The Gupta-era frescoes are considered some of the finest art of ancient India — inspiring artists and scholars worldwide.

✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Navaratna (Nine Gems) in Chandragupta II's court. Famous: Kalidasa (poet), Varahamihira (astronomer), Dhanvantari (physician), Amarasimha (lexicographer), Shanku (architecture). Peak of Golden Age.
Q7. Kalidasa, the greatest Sanskrit poet and dramatist, lived during which period?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Kalidasa (Gupta period, 4th-5th c. CE). Navaratna of Chandragupta II. Works: Shakuntala, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava, Ritusamhara. Sanskrit. 'Shakespeare of India.' C. 4th-5th century CE, court poet of Chandragupta II.
Q8. The Gupta Empire's capital was at which city (in modern Bihar)?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Pataliputra (Patna, Bihar) — continuing Mauryan and Shunga tradition. Chandragupta II also had Ujjain as second capital (after conquering Malwa). Centre of Golden Age.
Q9. Nalanda University, the great Buddhist centre of learning, was founded during the Gupta period by which ruler?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Kumargupta I founded Nalanda (c. 427 CE, near Rajgir, Bihar). 10,000 students, 2,000 teachers from China, Korea, Tibet, Central Asia. Destroyed by Bakhtiyar Khalji (~1200 CE). UNESCO (2016).
Q10. Fa-Hien (Faxian), the Chinese Buddhist pilgrim, visited India during which Gupta king's reign?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Fa-Hien visited during Chandragupta II's reign (405-411 CE). 'Fo-kwo-ki' describes Gupta India: peaceful, prosperous, low taxes, no capital punishment. Visited Lumbini, Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, Kushinagar.
Q11. The Gupta period is called the 'Golden Age' of ancient India. Which of the following is a key reason?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Golden Age: literature (Kalidasa), science (Aryabhata), medicine (Sushruta, Charaka), art (Ajanta), architecture (temples), metallurgy (Iron Pillar), religion (Hinduism + Buddhism). Extraordinary achievements across all fields.
Q12. Aryabhata, the great mathematician and astronomer, lived during the Gupta period. Which of the following is his contribution?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Aryabhata (c. 476-550 CE), Aryabhatiya (499 CE): pi (~3.1416), Earth rotation, eclipses (shadows), place-value, sine table. Age 23 when wrote it. India's first satellite named 'Aryabhata' (1975). He wrote the Aryabhatiya, calculated pi, stated the earth rotates on its axis, and explained eclipses.
Q13. The famous Ajanta Caves (Buddhist rock-cut caves with magnificent paintings) were mostly built during which period?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra, 29 caves) mostly Gupta period (4th-6th c. CE). Caves 16-27 have magnificent frescoes (Jataka tales). Some earlier (2nd c. BCE). UNESCO (1983). C. 4th-6th century CE, though some earlier.
Q14. The Iron Pillar of Delhi (Mehrauli) — famous for not rusting — was built during which period?
✅ Correct Answer: (B) — Iron Pillar of Delhi (Mehrauli) — Gupta period (Chandragupta II). ~7m tall, ~6 tons. Not rusted in ~1600 years. Sanskrit inscription praises 'Chandra' (Chandragupta II). Originally at Udayagiri. C. 4th century CE, during Chandragupta II's reign.
Q15. The Gupta Empire declined due to invasions by which foreign tribe?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Huns (White Huns / Hephthalites) from Central Asia (5th-6th c. CE). Skandagupta defeated them but treasury exhausted. Toramana and Mihirakula (c. 510-530 CE) finally broke Gupta power.
Q16. Skandagupta (c. 455-467 CE), the last great Gupta emperor, is known for which achievement?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Skandagupta (455-467 CE) defeated Huns + restored Sudarshana Lake (Junagadh inscription). But wars exhausted treasury (gold coins lighter). Empire fragmented after him.
Q17. The Gupta Empire followed which religion as its state religion?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Guptas = Brahminical Hinduism (Vaishnavism). Chandragupta II = 'Paramabhagavata' (supreme devotee of Vishnu). But tolerant — patronised Buddhism (Nalanda), Jainism.
Q18. The Gupta administration was characterized by which feature?
✅ Correct Answer: (A) — Gupta admin = decentralized. Feudatories (samantas), land grants (agraharas) to Brahmins/temples, provinces (bhuktis), districts (vishayas). This feudal structure eventually weakened the empire.
Q19. Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang), the Chinese Buddhist pilgrim, visited India during which period?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Hiuen Tsang visited during Harsha's reign (c. 630-645 CE, post-Gupta). 'Si-yu-ki.' Studied at Nalanda (Bihar) for 5 years. Met Harsha. Crucial source for post-Gupta India.
Q20. The position of women during the Gupta period was characterized by which feature?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Women's status declined: no property rights (except stridhana), child marriage, sati began (first record: Eran, 510 CE), no education. Upper-class women (queens) were educated (Kumaradevi).
Q21. The caste system during the Gupta period was characterized by which feature?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Caste system proliferated: new sub-castes (jatis) emerged (varna-sankara, occupational groups, absorption of foreigners). Untouchability increased (chandalas). Number of castes multiplied.
Q22. Bihar's connection with the Gupta Empire is primarily through which fact?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Bihar = Gupta heartland: (1) Pataliputra (Patna) was the Gupta capital. (2) Nalanda University founded by Kumargupta I (427 CE) in Bihar. (3) Guptas likely originated from Magadha (Bihar). Golden Age centred in Bihar!
Q23. The Guptas issued which type of coins primarily?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Guptas issued gold coins (dinaras) — largest gold coinage in ancient India. Portraits of kings (archer, tiger-slayer, lute-player) + titles. Silver (rupaka) after conquering Gujarat. Later coins became lighter (decline).
Q24. Which Gupta king is known as the 'musician king' — depicted playing the veena (lute) on his coins?
✅ Correct Answer: (C) — Samudragupta = 'musician king' (depicted playing veena on coins). Polymath: warrior (Napoleon of India), poet ('Kaviraja'), musician (veena), scholar. Allahabad inscription describes his talents.
Q25. The Gupta Era (starting c. 319-320 CE) is still used in India for which purpose?
✅ Correct Answer: (D) — Gupta Era (319-320 CE) was historically significant for dating inscriptions, grants, and texts for ~300+ years. While Saka Era (78 CE) is the official National Calendar today, the Gupta Era was widely used in ancient India.
